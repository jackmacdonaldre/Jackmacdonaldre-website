Kingsgate Kirkland: What Buyers Actually Find Here

By Jack Macdonald
Macdonald Group of Compass

A lot of people end up looking at Kingsgate because the price point makes sense relative to Kirkland's waterfront areas, and they want to know if the tradeoff is worth it. The short answer is yes, usually, but it depends on what matters to you. Let me walk through what I actually see when buyers are considering this area.

Where Kingsgate Sits and Why It Matters for Your Commute

Kingsgate is in the northeastern corner of Kirkland, bounded by I 405 to the west, the city limits to the north and east, and the Totem Lake Business District to the south and southeast. NE 116th Street marks the southern edge, and the neighborhood runs north from there past NE 132nd Street all the way to the city limits. That location puts it close to the Totem Lake area on one side and closer to Redmond and Bothell on the other. If you work in Redmond, particularly near the Microsoft campus, this is genuinely one of the more convenient parts of Kirkland to live. You can get to Redmond without touching the freeway most days.

For downtown Bellevue or Seattle commutes, you are looking at I 405 access via NE 124th Street or Totem Lake, which works fine outside peak hours. During rush hour, 405 through this stretch can back up, so if you are commuting south daily, factor that in honestly.

The Housing Stock

Most of Kingsgate was built in the 1970s to 1990s, with a mix of ramblers, split levels, and two story homes on reasonably sized lots. You will find a lot of 3 to 4 bedroom homes in the 1,800 to 2,600 square foot range. Lot sizes tend to run a bit larger here than in newer Eastside subdivisions, which is something buyers with kids or dogs often appreciate.

The age of these homes matters. A 1970s or 1980s house in Kingsgate that has not been updated is going to need attention: roofs, electrical panels, windows, kitchens. Some homes have been fully renovated and show well. Others have had cosmetic updates but aging systems underneath. When you are touring, look past the paint and countertops. The bones are usually solid, but the mechanicals tell the real story.

There are also some newer pockets, particularly townhomes and infill construction near the Totem Lake edge of the neighborhood. These give you a more modern layout but on much smaller lots, often with shared walls. Worth knowing before you assume every option here comes with a yard.

Kingsgate vs. Totem Lake vs. Juanita

These three areas come up together a lot, and buyers sometimes conflate them. Totem Lake is the commercial and medical hub just to the south and southeast, with the Village at Totem Lake and ongoing development activity in the area. Juanita is to the west, closer to Lake Washington, and tends to carry a price premium because of the lake access and the feel of the older tree lined streets near Juanita Beach Park.

Kingsgate falls in between. You do not get the lake proximity of Juanita, but you also are not paying for it. Compared to Totem Lake proper, Kingsgate tends to feel more purely residential and quieter at night. The tradeoff is that you are a little further from the lakefront and from downtown Kirkland, which some people genuinely do not care about and others find they miss more than they expected.

Schools

Kingsgate falls within the Lake Washington School District, which is well regarded on the Eastside. Elementary school assignments vary by address, so it is worth verifying where a specific home feeds before assuming. Robert Frost Elementary, Kirk Elementary, and Helen Keller Elementary are among the schools that serve this area, though boundary lines shift occasionally and not every address feeds the same school. Kamiakin Middle School and Finn Hill Middle School are the two middle schools in the Juanita High School feeder pattern, and Juanita High School is the typical high school assignment for this part of Kirkland. Use the Lake Washington School District's school and bus finder at lwsd.org to confirm the exact assignment for any specific address before purchasing.

What the Neighborhood Actually Feels Like Day to Day

Kingsgate is not a walkable neighborhood in the urban sense. There is no main street with coffee shops and restaurants within walking distance of most addresses. You are going to drive or bike to most errands. The Totem Lake area and the Fred Meyer on 120th Avenue NE are close, and the Costco and big box retail along 120th are convenient, but you should have realistic expectations about daily walkability.

What it does have is a quieter, more suburban feel with mature trees, decent lot sizes, and streets that are not heavily cut through. There is Kingsgate Park, a natural open space with trails, as well as 132nd Square Park, which has a synthetic turf playfield, playground, and picnic shelters. The neighborhoods are generally safe and calm for evening walks even if they are not particularly dense or interesting on foot.

What Buyers Sometimes Overlook

A few things I think are worth flagging explicitly:

  • Traffic noise near I 405 is real for homes that back up to the corridor. Some people are not bothered by it; others find it more than they expected. Worth a visit at different times of day.
  • The Totem Lake area has gone through significant redevelopment in recent years, with the Village at Totem Lake replacing the old mall, and planning for additional phases continuing. For Kingsgate buyers, this mostly means improved nearby amenities, but it also means ongoing activity and changing traffic patterns in spots.
  • Homes with original single pane windows from the 1970s are common and can significantly affect utility costs and comfort during cold months. Worth asking about specifically in older homes.
  • Some streets in Kingsgate have modest lot slopes, which is not a deal breaker but can complicate fencing, landscaping, and outdoor use if you are picturing a flat backyard.

Who Kingsgate Tends to Suit

It works well for people who want more house for the money than Juanita or downtown Kirkland offer, have a Redmond or Totem Lake area employer, and are not counting on walkability. Families who want a quieter street and a real yard tend to like it. If the energy of a more urban Kirkland neighborhood is what you are after, this part of town is probably not going to feel right, and that is an honest observation rather than a criticism of the area.

Common Questions

What school district covers Kingsgate Kirkland?

Kingsgate falls within the Lake Washington School District. Elementary assignments vary by exact address, so verify the specific school before purchasing if that matters to your decision. The district's school and bus finder at lwsd.org is the right tool for that. Juanita High School is the typical high school assignment for this area, fed by Kamiakin Middle School and Finn Hill Middle School, but boundary lines do change periodically.

How old are most homes in Kingsgate?

The majority of the neighborhood was built between the 1970s and 1990s. You will find some fully updated homes and some that still have original systems. Roofs, windows, electrical panels, and HVAC are worth scrutinizing carefully in any home from this era.

Is Kingsgate walkable?

Not really, at least not in the sense of walking to restaurants or shops regularly. Most daily errands require a car. The neighborhood itself is pleasant to walk around and has parks nearby, but it is a suburban area and you should plan around driving for most things.

How does Kingsgate pricing compare to other parts of Kirkland?

Kingsgate typically comes in below Juanita and downtown Kirkland, largely because it lacks lake access and proximity to the waterfront. The tradeoff is that you generally get more square footage and larger lots for the price. Exact figures shift with the market, so current comparable sales are the right reference point.

Is there anything to know about the Totem Lake area nearby?

Totem Lake has gone through significant redevelopment in recent years, replacing the old Totem Lake Mall with the Village at Totem Lake, a mixed use center with retail, restaurants, apartments, and public plazas. Planning for additional phases in the district continues. For Kingsgate buyers, this mostly means improved nearby amenities over time, but also some ongoing activity and traffic changes along 124th and around the Totem Lake area. It is worth driving through on a weekday to get a realistic sense of current conditions.

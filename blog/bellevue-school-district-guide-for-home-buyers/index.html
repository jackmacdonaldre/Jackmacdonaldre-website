Bellevue School District Guide for Home Buyers

By Jack Macdonald
Macdonald Group of Compass

Why BSD Comes Up in Almost Every Conversation

If you're buying a home in Bellevue or the surrounding area, the Bellevue School District comes up fast. Sometimes it's the first question, before we've even talked about price range or commute. That's not surprising given BSD's reputation, but I want to give you a more honest picture than the usual ranking site summary, because the way BSD shapes the housing market is more nuanced than "it's a great district, buy here."

Let me walk through what's actually useful to know.

What BSD Actually Covers

Bellevue School District doesn't map cleanly onto the city of Bellevue. It covers parts of Bellevue, Redmond, Kirkland, Sammamish, and Issaquah, and even small pockets of Newcastle. This surprises a lot of buyers who assume a Bellevue address automatically means BSD. It doesn't.

The reverse is also true: some homes with Redmond or Kirkland mailing addresses fall inside BSD boundaries. So the only reliable way to confirm which district and which school serve a specific property is to use the BSD boundary tool on the district's website and enter the actual address. Don't go by neighborhood name, don't go by the listing description, and don't go by what the neighbors say. Check the address directly.

How the Schools Are Organized

BSD runs a standard K through 5 elementary, 6 through 8 middle, and 9 through 12 high school structure. There are five comprehensive high schools: Bellevue, Newport, Sammamish, Interlake, and Skyline. Each serves a defined geographic zone, and which high school your address feeds into matters to a lot of families.

Beyond the neighborhood schools, BSD has a handful of choice and specialty programs that draw applicants from across the district. International School is probably the most well known, offering an IB curriculum starting in elementary school. Admission is competitive and not guaranteed by address. Interlake has a Science, Technology, Engineering, and Math focus that draws students districtwide. These programs are worth knowing about if you have specific educational goals for your kids, but they're separate from your neighborhood assignment and shouldn't be the primary reason you buy in a particular zone.

Are All the Schools Equally Strong?

BSD as a whole performs well on state and national benchmarks, but no, all the schools are not identical. There are real differences between elementary schools in particular, and those differences get discussed extensively in local parent communities.

Test scores, class sizes, and principal stability vary. The schools that consistently draw the most attention from buyers tend to be in the south Bellevue and Somerset areas, and some of the Sammamish feeders. But I'd push back on the idea that you should stretch your budget significantly just to land in one elementary school zone over another. Elementary attendance areas have shifted over time as the district has adjusted for enrollment changes. The school your neighbor's kid attended five years ago may not be the school your child attends.

Middle and high school boundaries are more stable, and those arguably matter more for longer term planning.

How BSD Affects Home Prices

BSD is one reason Bellevue carries a premium over some neighboring cities. But within BSD, there are meaningful price differences tied to school zone. Homes that feed into Newport High School have historically commanded strong interest. Homes in the Interlake and Bellevue High zones vary more depending on location and housing stock.

What I see play out regularly is buyers paying more than they expected for a home partly because of the school assignment, then realizing a few years later that the school was fine either way. The premium is real. Whether it's worth it depends on how strongly you weight school assignment relative to other factors like lot size, commute, or the home itself.

It's also worth knowing that BSD covers enough geography that you can often find a solid home in the district at a range of price points. The expensive assumption is that only the most sought after addresses inside BSD count. That's not really true.

Open Enrollment and Boundary Exceptions

BSD does allow some flexibility through open enrollment, which lets families apply to schools outside their attendance area if space is available. This is not guaranteed and depends on capacity year to year. If you're buying with the intention of open enrolling your child somewhere other than the neighborhood school, understand that this plan can change. Build your decision around where your address actually sends your kids, not where you hope to transfer them.

A Few Things That Don't Show Up in the Rankings

Commute to school is worth thinking about practically. BSD is geographically spread out, and if your elementary school is more than a mile or two from home, you're either driving or figuring out the bus schedule. The district does provide bus service, but not universally to every student at every school, and the routes and eligibility rules are worth reviewing directly with the district before you assume transportation is handled.

Also, the district has invested heavily in facilities in recent years following bond measures, so the physical condition of buildings is generally good across BSD. That's not always the case in every district in the region, and it matters for day to day experience even if it doesn't show up in test score tables.

What to Actually Do Before You Make an Offer

Look up the specific address on the BSD boundary locator. Confirm the elementary, middle, and high school assignments. If you have strong feelings about a particular school, drive by it, look up recent GreatSchools or Niche profiles with some skepticism about what those scores actually measure, and if possible talk to parents in the neighborhood. That last one tells you more than any ranking.

If you're cross shopping BSD against Lake Washington School District or Issaquah School District, all three are strong, and the honest answer is that for most families the differences matter less than proximity to work, the home itself, and what you can realistically afford. BSD has the strongest name recognition in the region, but that name recognition is partly why Bellevue costs what it costs.

Common Questions

Does a Bellevue mailing address always mean Bellevue School District?

No, and this is one of the most common misconceptions buyers run into. City limits and school district boundaries don't match up. Some Bellevue addresses fall in Lake Washington School District or even Issaquah School District. The only reliable way to confirm is to enter the specific property address into the BSD boundary locator on the district's official website.

What are the five high schools in BSD and does it matter which one a home feeds into?

The five comprehensive high schools are Bellevue, Newport, Sammamish, Interlake, and Skyline. It does matter to many buyers, and Newport in particular tends to draw the most attention from families. That attention is reflected in home prices in that zone. Whether the differences in outcomes between the high schools justify the price premium is something each family has to decide for themselves, but they're all functioning well resourced schools.

Can I choose a different school within BSD than my address is assigned to?

BSD does have an open enrollment process, but spaces are limited and availability changes year to year. It's not something you can count on when you're planning a purchase. If a specific school is a priority, buy in that attendance area rather than assuming you'll transfer in later.

How do BSD schools compare to Lake Washington School District?

Both are well regarded and competitive with each other on most measures. BSD has stronger name recognition, which is a real factor in home pricing. Lake Washington SD covers Kirkland, Redmond, and parts of Sammamish, and its schools perform at a similar level in most categories. Buyers who need to be closer to the 520 corridor or who find better value in Kirkland or Redmond sometimes end up in LWSD and are generally satisfied with it.

Does BSD provide bus transportation to all students?

Not universally. Eligibility for bus service is based on distance from school and grade level, and routes can change. If daily transportation logistics matter to your family, contact the district directly before closing on a home rather than assuming the bus will cover your address.

How stable are school attendance boundaries in BSD?

Boundaries do shift, particularly at the elementary level, as enrollment patterns change across the district. High school boundaries have been more stable historically. If you're making a long term plan based on a specific school assignment, it's worth knowing that the assignment your address carries today may not be permanent, especially at the elementary level.

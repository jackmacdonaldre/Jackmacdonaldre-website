# Totem Lake Kirkland: What Buyers Should Know

> Thinking about buying in Totem Lake Kirkland? Here is what the housing stock, commute, and neighborhood feel actually look like for buyers.

## Most people ask about Totem Lake because of the price point

That is usually the entry point. Someone is looking at Kirkland, sees the numbers in Juanita or downtown Kirkland, and then finds something in Totem Lake that fits their budget better. That is a reasonable way to start, but price alone does not tell you what you are actually buying into. The neighborhood has changed a lot in the last several years, and there are real tradeoffs worth understanding before you make an offer.

## What the housing stock actually looks like

Totem Lake is not a uniform neighborhood. The western side, closer to 116th Avenue NE, has older single family homes built mostly in the 1970s and 1980s. These tend to sit on decent sized lots, often a quarter acre or more, and the homes themselves are typical of that era: split levels, daylight basements, wood siding, and varying levels of renovation. If you are comparing to Juanita or Finn Hill, the lots can be comparable but the homes skew older and the streets feel more suburban and less established.

The eastern side of Totem Lake, particularly around The Village at Totem Lake and NE 124th Street, is where the newer development is concentrated. There have been significant condo and townhome projects added in recent years, and more are still being built or planned. These are mostly attached units, some with HOA fees that run a few hundred dollars a month. They appeal to buyers who want newer construction without going all the way to Redmond or Bothell.

Condos in this area generally range from studios and one bedrooms up to two or three bedroom units. The older Totem Lake condo complexes from the 1980s and 1990s are priced lower but come with the usual considerations around reserves, deferred maintenance, and rental cap restrictions that can affect your financing options.

## The mall redevelopment and what it means day to day

Totem Lake Mall spent years as a struggling retail center, but it has been substantially redeveloped into what is now called The Village at Totem Lake. There is a mix of retail, restaurants, medical offices, and new housing around it. For buyers, this matters because it has changed the walkability of the immediate area. If you are in one of the newer condos or townhomes near 120th Avenue NE, you have grocery, dining, and services within a reasonable walk or a very short drive. For the older single family homes further west or north, it is still pretty car dependent.

The honest version is that Totem Lake is not Kirkland's waterfront or Carillon Point. It does not have that kind of walkable character. But it also does not pretend to be, and buyers who come in with realistic expectations tend to be satisfied with what they get.

## Commute and location

This is actually one of Totem Lake's stronger points. The neighborhood sits close to SR 522 and has reasonable access to I 405 via NE 124th Street. If you are commuting to Bellevue, you are looking at 15 to 25 minutes most days depending on where you exit. Redmond is very close, maybe 10 to 15 minutes to the main tech campuses. Seattle is further, and 405 south through Bellevue can be rough during peak hours.

The I 405 express toll lanes run through this stretch of Kirkland, and there is a direct access ramp at NE 128th Street near the northern edge of the neighborhood. Whether the toll lanes help your specific commute depends on where you are headed and which on ramp you use, so it is worth checking your actual route.

There is also direct bus service on some routes toward Redmond and Bellevue, and the area is part of the broader Kirkland network. It is not a neighborhood where you would go car free, but transit is not useless either.

## Schools

Totem Lake falls within Lake Washington School District, which is well regarded across the board. School assignments depend on your exact address within the neighborhood, and boundaries have shifted in recent years, so the best move is to run your specific address through the LWSD school locator at lwsd.org before relying on any general description. Middle school has typically been Finn Hill Middle School or Kirkland Middle School depending on where in the neighborhood you land, and the high school for most of the area is Juanita High School. These are strong schools. If you are comparing to what you would get in Redmond or Sammamish within the same district, you are largely in the same situation.

## What buyers typically have to weigh

The main tradeoff in Totem Lake is straightforward. You get more for your money than you would in central Kirkland or Juanita, but you are giving something up in terms of neighborhood polish and walkability to the kind of amenities that make Kirkland's downtown area appealing. The older single family homes here need more scrutiny during inspection. Roofs, electrical panels, and drainage are the things I would look at closely in a 1970s or 1980s home in this area.

For condos, the key questions are HOA financial health and rental restrictions. Some of the older complexes in Totem Lake have high rental percentages, which can complicate conventional financing. If you are planning to put less than 20 percent down, your lender will want to look at the condo's approval status carefully. FHA and VA financing can be tricky in complexes that do not meet the occupancy ratios. This is not unique to Totem Lake, but it comes up here more often because of the mix of older condo stock.

The newer townhomes are generally cleaner on the financing side, but they come with HOA fees and sometimes shared walls that not every buyer is comfortable with.

## Who tends to do well here

Totem Lake works best for buyers who are commuting to the Eastside or Redmond, want more space than they can afford in central Kirkland, and are okay trading some neighborhood character for a better price per square foot. It also suits buyers who specifically want new construction condos or townhomes and do not need a single family lot. And it makes sense for people who want Kirkland as their address and Lake Washington School District without paying Juanita or Rose Hill prices.

It is a less obvious fit for buyers who prioritize walkability or who want a neighborhood that already feels fully realized. Some parts of Totem Lake are still in process, with construction nearby and a retail and dining scene that is improving but not complete.

## Common Questions

### Are the newer condos and townhomes in Totem Lake part of an HOA?

Almost all of them, yes. HOA fees vary by complex depending on what is covered and whether there are shared amenities, so you will want to review the specific numbers for any building you are considering. Before making an offer, ask for the HOA's financial documents and reserve fund status. Underfunded reserves in an older complex are a real cost risk.

### How does Totem Lake compare to Juanita for price?

Generally speaking, Totem Lake comes in lower. Juanita has stronger neighborhood identity, proximity to Lake Washington, and more desirable single family streets, so it commands a premium. The gap is not always enormous, but it is consistent enough that buyers on a tighter budget often end up looking at Totem Lake after pricing out of Juanita.

### Is the Totem Lake Mall area actually improving or just under construction?

It has genuinely improved. The redevelopment into The Village at Totem Lake brought in Whole Foods Market, Trader Joe's, some restaurants, and medical and professional tenants. It still does not have the density or variety of a true walkable retail district, but it is meaningfully more functional than it was five or ten years ago.

### Can I get FHA financing on a condo in Totem Lake?

It depends on the specific complex. FHA loans require the condo project to be approved, and older complexes sometimes have too high a percentage of rental units to qualify. If FHA or VA financing matters to you, check the complex's approval status before you get too far into the process. Your lender can help with this, but do not wait until the last minute.

### What is the realistic commute from Totem Lake to South Lake Union or downtown Seattle?

Plan on 40 to 60 minutes on a typical weekday morning depending on the time you leave and whether you are driving or taking transit. It is not a short commute. If you are going to Seattle every day, that is worth factoring into the decision honestly, because the savings on housing can erode when you account for time and transportation costs.

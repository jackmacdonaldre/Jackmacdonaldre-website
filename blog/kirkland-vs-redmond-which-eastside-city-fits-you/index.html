Kirkland vs Redmond: Which Eastside City Fits You?

By Jack Macdonald
Macdonald Group of Compass

People ask me this one constantly, usually right after they've started looking at homes on the Eastside and realized that Kirkland and Redmond keep showing up in their search. The prices look similar, the commute times to Seattle or Bellevue aren't wildly different, and both cities have good schools. So why does one feel so different from the other when you actually spend time there?

The honest answer is that they serve different lives. Not better or worse, just different. Here's how I'd actually explain it to someone sitting across from me.

What Kirkland Is Actually Like to Live In

Kirkland has Lake Washington on its western edge, and that shapes everything. Downtown Kirkland, particularly around Marina Park and the waterfront, has a walkability and energy that most Eastside cities simply don't have. You can walk to dinner, walk to a coffee shop, walk to the water. That's genuinely rare out here.

The tradeoff is that Kirkland is hilly and irregular in a way that can be inconvenient. Streets don't follow a tidy grid. Getting from one neighborhood to another often means dropping down to a main road and working your way back up. Juanita, Totem Lake, Bridle Trails, South Kirkland, and the downtown core all feel like distinct pockets rather than one continuous city.

Housing stock is older in most of Kirkland. A lot of the single family homes you'll find in the central and southern parts of the city were built in the 1970s to 1980s. That means smaller lot coverage, more mature trees, some funkier floor plans, and more deferred maintenance situations than you'd find in newer construction. The closer you get to the water or downtown, the more expensive it gets, often significantly.

Juanita is worth calling out specifically because buyers sometimes overlook it. It has its own beach park on Lake Washington, a neighborhood feel that's a little quieter than downtown Kirkland, and prices that are often more accessible while still being in the Lake Washington School District.

What Redmond Is Actually Like to Live In

Redmond feels more planned, more suburban in the traditional sense, and in a lot of ways more functional on a day to day basis. The street layout is easier. There are more newer homes, particularly in areas like Education Hill, Idylwood, and the newer developments closer to Overlake. If you want a 2000s or 2010s build with a more conventional floor plan and good storage, Redmond has more of those.

Downtown Redmond has been improving. The Redmond Town Center area and the blocks around it have more retail and restaurant options than they did five or ten years ago, and the light rail extension to Redmond Technology Station has changed how some people think about getting around. That said, it still doesn't have the same walkable character as downtown Kirkland. It's more of a destination you drive to than a place you wander around.

The other big thing about Redmond is the tech employment concentration. Microsoft's main campus is there. Amazon, Meta, and others have offices in the Overlake corridor. If that's where you work, living in Redmond can simplify your life considerably. Some people genuinely never need to get on I 405 or SR 520.

Schools: Both Are Strong, but Check the Specific Assignment

Most of Kirkland falls within the Lake Washington School District, which is well regarded. Redmond is also largely in the Lake Washington School District, so for a lot of buyers the district comparison is a wash. The difference tends to come down to which specific school a given home feeds into, which can vary by street within the same neighborhood. If a particular school matters to you, look up the actual boundary for the specific address before you get attached to a home.

A small number of homes in certain parts of Kirkland fall within the Bellevue School District rather than Lake Washington. School district boundaries do not always follow city limits, so if you're looking anywhere in Kirkland and the district assignment matters to you, confirm the exact address with the district's school locator tool before making assumptions.

The Price Difference (and What You Actually Get for It)

Kirkland waterfront and near water properties carry a significant premium, as you'd expect. But if you're comparing a standard single family home in Kirkland's inland neighborhoods to a comparable home in Redmond, the gap is narrower than most people assume. You might find slightly more square footage or a newer build in Redmond for the same price, but Kirkland's location advantage near the lake and the established feel of its neighborhoods holds value.

What you're really trading in Kirkland is updates and newness for location and character. What you're trading in Redmond is walkability and water access for practicality and often a cleaner renovation situation.

Commute Realities

To Seattle via SR 520, Kirkland has a slight edge for most of the city since you're a little closer to the bridge. Redmond to Seattle during peak hours can add meaningful time, particularly from the eastern parts of Redmond that are farther from the freeway.

To Bellevue, both cities are reasonably comparable depending on exactly where you live, though Redmond's connections to I 405 and SR 520 are fairly direct from most of the city.

To Bellevue's Spring District or to Overlake specifically, Redmond wins without much debate, especially now with light rail connecting those corridors.

Who Tends to End Up in Each Place

This is a generalization, but it reflects what I see. Buyers who prioritize walkability, water access, and neighborhood character tend to gravitate toward Kirkland, even if they have to compromise on square footage or take on a home that needs work. Buyers who prioritize newer construction, easy access to tech campuses, and a more straightforward suburban setup often land in Redmond.

Families with young kids sometimes prefer Redmond's flatter terrain and the prevalence of sidewalks and parks in areas like Education Hill. Kirkland's hills can make it less casual for bikes or strollers depending on where you are. That said, Kirkland's parks system along the water is genuinely excellent for families who are willing to drive a few minutes to reach it.

Neither city is a wrong answer. But if you're trying to figure out which one actually fits your life, the honest exercise is to spend a Saturday morning in each one doing what you'd normally do on a weekend. The difference becomes obvious pretty quickly.

Common Questions

Is downtown Kirkland actually walkable or is that overstated?

It's real, but it applies mainly to the downtown core and the neighborhoods immediately around it. Within a half mile or so of Marina Park and the central retail area, you can genuinely walk to dinner, coffee, groceries, and the waterfront. Once you move into Juanita, Totem Lake, or Bridle Trails, you're back to driving for most errands. So yes, Kirkland is walkable in spots, but it's not evenly walkable across the whole city.

Does living in Redmond make sense if I don't work at a tech company?

Completely. The tech employment base inflates demand a bit, but it doesn't define who lives there. Redmond has good parks, solid schools, and a reasonable suburban setup that suits all kinds of households. The proximity to tech campuses just happens to be a bonus for some buyers.

Which city has more new construction available?

Redmond generally has more newer single family homes and townhomes, particularly in the Overlake area and in newer developments on Education Hill. Kirkland has some newer construction too, but the older housing stock is more dominant there, especially in the central and southern neighborhoods.

How much more expensive is Kirkland waterfront compared to a standard home in either city?

The premium can be substantial. Homes with direct lake access or strong water views in Kirkland routinely sell at a significant markup compared to comparable square footage just a few blocks inland. The exact gap varies a lot by specific location and condition, but buyers sometimes underestimate how wide that spread is until they start comparing actives side by side.

What about Totem Lake? I keep seeing homes listed there but am not sure what it's like.

Totem Lake is in the northern part of Kirkland and has historically been more of a commercial and light industrial area. There has been meaningful redevelopment in recent years, with new housing and retail coming in. It's more affordable than other parts of Kirkland, but it doesn't have the same neighborhood feel as central Kirkland or Juanita. Worth considering if price is a priority, but go spend some time there before committing.

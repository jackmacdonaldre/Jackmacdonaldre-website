# Moving to Bellevue from California: What to Actually Expect

> Thinking about relocating from California to Bellevue, WA? Here's what actually changes, from taxes to weather to housing costs, explained plainly.

Author: Jack Macdonald, REALTOR, Macdonald Group of Compass, Bellevue WA
Published: August 31, 2026
Area: Bellevue, Washington
Web page: https://jackmacdonaldre.com/blog/moving-to-bellevue-from-california-a-practical-guide/

Every few weeks I talk to someone who's made the move from California, or is seriously considering it. The reasons vary: a job at Microsoft or Amazon, remote work flexibility, family in the area, or just the sense that they've been priced out of wherever they're living. Whatever brought them here, they tend to arrive with the same set of questions. This is my attempt to answer them honestly.

## The Tax Picture Is Real, But It's More Complicated Than People Think

Washington has no state income tax on wages today. (A 9.9 percent tax on income above $1 million was signed in March 2026, but it does not start until 2028 and faces a legal challenge.) For someone coming from California, where the top marginal rate can hit 13.3%, that's a significant change. On a $200,000 salary, the difference is meaningful enough that it factors into a lot of relocation decisions, and employers here know it too.

What you give up is a relatively low sales tax rate. Washington's combined state and local sales tax in Bellevue is 10.3%. California's base rate is lower, though it adds up depending on the county. Property taxes in Washington are lower than California in some ways and comparable in others, but the assessment structure is different enough that you shouldn't assume either direction without running actual numbers on a specific home.

The other thing California transplants sometimes miss: Washington has a 7 percent capital gains tax on long term gains from certain assets above an inflation adjusted standard deduction ($278,000 for 2025), with an extra 2.9 percent on gains above $1 million starting with 2025. It passed in 2021 and was upheld by the state Supreme Court in 2023. It doesn't apply to real estate sales, but if you're selling stock or other assets as part of your move, worth knowing before you assume it's a fully no income tax state in every dimension.

## What Your Housing Budget Actually Gets You Here

If you're coming from San Francisco, San Jose, or most of Los Angeles, Bellevue will feel expensive but not shocking. If you're coming from Sacramento, Fresno, or the Inland Empire, the sticker prices here will feel significant.

The Eastside (Bellevue, Kirkland, Redmond, Sammamish) has seen sustained demand because of the tech employment base. A single family home in Bellevue proper typically starts well above a million dollars. That's a real number, not a rounding error.

What you're getting for that: larger lots than you'd see in comparable Bay Area pricing, newer construction in many pockets, excellent public schools, and generally lower density than urban California neighborhoods at similar price points. The tradeoff is that you are still spending a lot of money, and the Seattle area has had meaningful price appreciation over the past decade, so the "affordable alternative to California" framing only goes so far.

Condos and townhomes fill out the lower end of the market. Areas like Factoria, Crossroads, and parts of northeast Bellevue tend to offer more entry points than the West Bellevue neighborhoods closer to Downtown.

## The Neighborhoods Within Bellevue Feel Pretty Different From Each Other

Bellevue is not uniform. Downtown Bellevue has become a genuinely walkable urban core with high rise condos, the Bellevue Collection shopping district, restaurants along Bellevue Way, and the Lincoln Square towers. It feels more city, like than most people expect before they visit.

Medina, Clyde Hill, and West Bellevue sit just west of Downtown and are some of the most expensive residential real estate in the state. These are established, quiet, and very much single family home territory.

The Eastgate and Factoria areas are more suburban in feel, closer to I 90, and offer a mix of older ranches, townhomes, and some newer construction. Families who want good schools without the highest possible price point often land here.

Crossroads is a neighborhood worth knowing about if you want genuine cultural diversity and more accessible pricing. The area around Crossroads Mall has an international food scene that regulars take seriously, and the housing tends to be more affordable relative to the rest of Bellevue.

Somerset and Newport Hills, in the southeast part of Bellevue, offer hillside homes with views in some cases, proximity to Cougar Mountain Regional Wildland Park, and a slightly slower pace than the urban core.

## Commute and Getting Around

This is where California transplants sometimes have the most adjusting to do, though not necessarily in the direction they expect. The Seattle metro does not have the freeway capacity to match its growth. I 405 running north, south through Bellevue gets congested in ways that can genuinely surprise people. Trips that look like 20 minutes on a map at 2pm can take 45 to 50 minutes at 5pm.

The good news: the East Link light rail extension connecting Redmond, Bellevue, and downtown Seattle was a significant infrastructure addition, and for people working in South Lake Union or downtown Seattle, it changes the calculus considerably. If your job is in Redmond, Microsoft's campus has its own transit infrastructure and many employees bike the Sammamish River Trail in warmer months.

Within Bellevue itself, getting around without a car is doable in some areas, particularly near Downtown, and much harder in others. It's not the Bay Area in terms of transit density, but it's improved.

## The Weather Is Not What People Fear, But It Is Real

Californians often brace for brutal winters. What they actually get is grey. October through March tends to be overcast, drizzly, and mild rather than cold and snowy. Snow in Bellevue is infrequent enough that the city occasionally shuts down when it does happen, which is its own cultural adjustment.

The summers are genuinely exceptional. June through September is often clear, dry, and comfortable in the 70s. The first summer almost always converts people. The question is whether they can stay converted through February.

Seasonal Affective Disorder is a real thing here, and people talk about it openly. If you've relied on sunlight for mood regulation and have never lived through a long Pacific Northwest winter, it's worth going in clear eyed. Light therapy lamps are not a quirky wellness trend here, they're just a thing people use.

## The Tech Economy Shapes Everything

Microsoft is headquartered in Redmond, a few miles from Bellevue. Amazon's main presence is in Seattle, but has significant operations throughout the region. There's a dense ecosystem of tech companies, startups, and supporting industries that makes the Eastside employment market feel different from most metros.

This also shapes the community in ways that aren't just economic. A significant portion of the population came here from somewhere else, which means it's relatively easy to meet people who are also new. There's also a large South Asian and East Asian professional community, particularly in Bellevue and Redmond, that shows up in everything from the restaurant landscape to the schools.

If you're not in tech, the area still functions fine. Healthcare, construction, retail, and service industries all exist and employ a lot of people. But the baseline salaries and housing prices have been shaped substantially by the tech sector, so it's worth factoring that into your housing budget expectations regardless of what you do.

## Schools and Why They Matter Even If You Don't Have Kids Yet

The Bellevue School District consistently ranks among the strongest in the state. This is not just perception; the test scores and graduation outcomes back it up. For families with kids, it's often the primary reason they end up on the Eastside rather than Seattle proper.

It also matters for resale value. Homes in top rated school districts hold value differently than comparable homes elsewhere. If you're buying and thinking about your exit at some point, the school district your home falls in does affect your buyer pool later.

## Practical Things People Wish They'd Known Earlier

Car tabs cost more here than most places. Sound Transit's regional motor vehicle excise tax (1.1 percent of your vehicle's depreciated value), combined with other state and local fees, produces registration fees that regularly surprise new residents.

Home inspection matters more than in some markets. A meaningful portion of the housing stock in parts of Bellevue was built in the 1970s and 1980s, and deferred maintenance can be hidden under renovation. Don't skip it to compete on an offer.

The offer process here has historically been competitive in a way that requires some strategy. Multiple offers and escalation clauses are common in the spring market. If you're used to making lowball offers in a slower California market, the approach needs to adjust.

Most Californians also underestimate how much of daily life here happens outdoors in ways that require gear rather than just showing up. Trail running, kayaking, skiing at Snoqualmie Pass (about 45 minutes from Bellevue on I 90), hiking in the Cascades. The outdoor access is a legitimate quality of life asset, and people who engage with it tend to adapt to the weather much better than people who don't.

## Common Questions

### Is Bellevue actually more affordable than the Bay Area for housing?

For comparable properties, Bellevue tends to be less expensive than San Francisco or San Jose, but the gap is smaller than people expect and has narrowed over the past decade. If you're comparing to Sacramento or San Diego, Bellevue can actually feel more expensive in many segments. The income tax savings help the affordability equation, but they don't close the gap on their own.

### How hard is it to get into the Bellevue School District?

If you live within the district boundaries, your kids attend BSD schools. The district covers most of Bellevue proper along with some surrounding areas. The thing to verify when buying is which specific school your address feeds into, since individual school quality within the district does vary even if the district overall is strong.

### Do I need to worry about earthquakes moving from California?

The Pacific Northwest sits on the Cascadia Subduction Zone, which is capable of producing very large earthquakes. This is a real risk that is discussed seriously in emergency planning circles, though major seismic events are infrequent. If you've lived in California, you already understand earthquake risk. Washington's building codes have improved significantly, but it's worth understanding the regional geology before assuming you're moving somewhere geologically calmer.

### What's the rental market like if I want to rent before buying?

Renting for six to twelve months before buying is genuinely smart in this market if you're not certain which area fits your lifestyle. Bellevue has a solid apartment and rental home inventory, particularly near Downtown and in neighborhoods like Crossroads. Rents are high by national standards but give you time to understand which neighborhoods actually suit how you live, which is worth the cost of waiting.

### Will I feel culture shock moving from California to Bellevue?

Probably less than you'd expect. Bellevue is a diverse, internationally connected city with a lot of transplants. The cultural adjustment is more about weather rhythms and pace than anything political or social. People here tend to be outdoors oriented, and there's a strong coffee shop culture that makes the grey months more bearable. The biggest shock for most Californians is just how dark and overcast the winters are.

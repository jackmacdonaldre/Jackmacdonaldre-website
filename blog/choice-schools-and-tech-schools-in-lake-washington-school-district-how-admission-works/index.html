Lake Washington School District Choice and Tech Schools Explained

By Jack Macdonald
Macdonald Group of Compass

One of the first questions I hear from families moving to Redmond or anywhere else in the Lake Washington School District is some version of: "We heard there are special schools you can apply to. How does that actually work?" It comes up constantly, and the honest answer is that the system is genuinely good but genuinely confusing if you don't know where to start.

Lake Washington School District (LWSD) runs what most districts don't: a real network of choice schools and specialized programs that go beyond your assigned neighborhood school. These aren't private schools or charters. They're public, tuition free, and open to any student who lives within the district. But getting in isn't automatic, and the timelines matter more than most families realize until they've already missed something.

What "Choice School" Actually Means Here

In LWSD, a choice school is a public school with a specific instructional focus or philosophy that families opt into rather than being assigned to by address. You apply, and if there are more applicants than seats, admission is determined by lottery. Siblings of currently enrolled students typically get priority at the entry grade level, which is worth knowing if you have more than one child you're thinking about enrolling.

The district's choice options have shifted over the years, so it's worth going directly to the LWSD website to confirm what's currently available for each grade band. That said, the programs that have been consistently part of the choice landscape include environmental and STEM focused options, a Spanish and English dual language program at the elementary level, and the flagship STEM program at the high school level.

The STEM School: What It Is and Who It's For

The most talked about piece of this is Tesla STEM High School, the district's science, technology, engineering and mathematics choice high school located in Redmond. It draws families from across the district, not just from one corner of it.

Tesla STEM isn't a vocational program in the traditional sense. The curriculum integrates science, technology, engineering and mathematics into a college prep framework using problem based learning. Students still take standard core courses. The difference is in how those courses connect to applied STEM projects and research, and in the culture of the school, which tends to attract students who are genuinely interested in building and making things rather than just consuming technology.

Admission to Tesla STEM is competitive, but not in the way a private school is competitive. There's no entrance exam or portfolio review for the lottery itself. You apply during the choice school enrollment window, and if demand exceeds capacity, a lottery is held. Where things get more selective is in specific pathways or courses within the school, some of which have prerequisites or teacher recommendations attached.

At the middle school level, LWSD also offers several choice schools with distinct instructional approaches, including options focused on environmental education, international studies, and arts integrated learning. None of these carry the same STEM only focus as Tesla at the high school level, but families should check the district's current choice schools page for the full list of what is available at each grade band, since programs do change.

The Enrollment Calendar Is the Thing Most Families Miss

Choice school applications in LWSD typically open in early November for enrollment the following school year, with applications due around December 1. Lotteries are generally held in January, with families notified shortly after. That window is short, usually about a month, and it's easy to miss if you move to the area after it's closed.

If you're relocating to Redmond or Kirkland and your move happens to fall outside that window, your child's options for the current year may be limited to the neighborhood school. The choice school waitlists do move sometimes, but you shouldn't count on it. Families who are serious about a particular program tend to plan their move timing around the enrollment calendar, which sounds extreme until you realize how much these programs matter to some kids.

The district holds informational nights for choice schools, and I'd genuinely encourage families to attend rather than rely entirely on the website. The principals and program coordinators do a good job of explaining what the day to day experience actually looks like, which helps you figure out whether the fit is real or just appealing on paper.

Neighborhood School vs. Choice School: The Real Tradeoff

Something worth saying directly: the neighborhood schools in LWSD are strong. This district consistently ranks among the top performing in Washington state, and a kid who goes to their assigned school is not getting a lesser education by default. The choice schools serve a specific purpose for families who want a particular environment or instructional approach, not for families who think the alternative is inferior.

The practical tradeoff with choice schools is transportation. Parents and guardians of students who attend choice schools are responsible for arranging their own transportation. The district does offer limited shuttle bus service to some choice schools in some cases, but it is not guaranteed and may involve a fee and its own lottery. Families should contact the specific choice school they are interested in to ask about what transportation options currently exist. For families in, say, the Sammamish area applying to a school in Redmond or Kirkland, the logistics are real and worth thinking through before the lottery, not after.

What This Means When You're Choosing Where to Live

Some families deliberately choose a neighborhood based on its proximity to a choice school they're targeting. That's a reasonable strategy, but it comes with a caveat: admission isn't guaranteed. I've worked with buyers who planned their entire search around a specific program, didn't get the lottery slot, and then weren't wild about their neighborhood school. It's worth having a backup position on where you'd be happy if the choice school doesn't work out.

On the other side, there are buyers who assume their assigned school will be the thing that matters most, then discover their kid qualifies for or is passionate about a program across town. According to the district's own map, LWSD covers 76 square miles and serves the cities of Kirkland and Redmond, as well as about half of Sammamish, so "across town" can mean a real commute.

If schools are central to your housing search and you're narrowing down areas, it's worth a direct conversation with the district's enrollment office rather than relying on third party school rating sites. Those sites often lag behind changes in program availability, and they don't capture the culture and environment of a school, which is often what families are really trying to evaluate.

A Few Practical Notes Before You Apply

You must be a resident of the Lake Washington School District to participate in choice school enrollment. The district boundaries don't perfectly match city limits, so if you're near the edges of Redmond, Kirkland, or Sammamish, it's worth verifying your address falls within LWSD and not a neighboring district before you plan around it.

Siblings of currently enrolled choice school students typically receive enrollment priority at the entry grade level. This is significant for families with more than one child: getting one kid into a program can meaningfully improve the odds for a younger sibling down the road. That said, sibling priority policies can vary by school, so it's worth confirming the specific policy with whichever program you're interested in.

Finally, the landscape of which specific schools carry which programs does change. New programs get added, some get restructured, and grade configurations shift. The district's official enrollment page is the right source, not what a neighbor told you worked three years ago.

Common Questions

Do you have to live in a specific neighborhood to apply to a choice school in LWSD?

No. Any student who lives within the Lake Washington School District boundaries can apply to any choice school, regardless of which neighborhood they live in. The lottery is open districtwide. The main thing to verify is that your home address actually falls within LWSD, since the district boundaries don't always align neatly with city limits.

Is there an academic requirement to get into the STEM school?

For the lottery itself, no. Admission to Tesla STEM High School goes through the same choice school enrollment process as other choice schools, and a lottery determines placement when there are more applicants than seats. However, once a student is enrolled, some specific courses or pathways within the school may have prerequisites or teacher recommendations attached.

What happens if we move to the area after the choice school enrollment window closes?

Your options for that school year may be limited to your assigned neighborhood school. Choice school waitlists exist and do move occasionally, but there's no guarantee. Families relocating with a strong interest in a specific program often try to time their move so they're enrolled in the district before the enrollment window opens, which typically happens in early November with applications due around December 1.

Does the district provide transportation to choice schools?

Generally, parents and guardians are responsible for arranging their own transportation to choice schools. The district does offer limited shuttle bus service to some choice schools in some cases, but availability is not guaranteed and details vary by school and year. Contact the specific choice school you are interested in for current transportation information.

If one of my kids gets into a choice school, does that help my younger child get in later?

Yes, sibling priority is a real factor in LWSD choice school enrollment. Siblings of currently enrolled students typically receive enrollment preference at the entry grade level during the application process, which improves the odds considerably compared to applying without any existing connection to the school. The specifics can vary by program, so it's worth confirming the sibling policy directly with the school you're interested in.

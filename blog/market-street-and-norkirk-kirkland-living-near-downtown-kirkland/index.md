# Norkirk and Market Street: Kirkland Living Close In

> Thinking about Norkirk or the Market Street area in Kirkland? Here is what it actually feels like to live there, what the housing stock looks like, and what tradeoffs to expect.

One of the most common questions I get from buyers interested in Kirkland is some version of: "How close can I get to downtown without paying lakefront prices?" Norkirk is usually my answer to that question.

The Norkirk neighborhood sits just north and slightly inland from downtown Kirkland, centered roughly around Market Street as it runs up from the waterfront. It is not a marketed subdivision or a planned community. It is just an older, established residential area that happens to be close enough to Lake Street and the waterfront that you can walk there in ten or fifteen minutes without it being a stretch.

## What Market Street Actually Is

Market Street is both a neighborhood anchor and a practical corridor. It runs north from the downtown core up through the Norkirk area, and along the way you get a mix of coffee shops, small restaurants, a few local businesses, and the kind of everyday errands that make a neighborhood feel usable rather than purely residential. It is not a sprawling commercial strip. The retail is concentrated, and it transitions pretty quickly into the residential streets that make up most of Norkirk.

Peter Kirk Park sits right at the edge of this area, just next to downtown, and is a real asset for families and anyone who wants green space without driving somewhere. It has a baseball field, a seasonal outdoor pool, a skate park, basketball and pickleball courts, and enough room that it does not feel crowded on a summer afternoon. That park, combined with the walkable stretch of Market Street, is a big part of why this area gets so much attention from buyers who want some urban convenience without being in an apartment building.

## The Housing Stock Is Older and That Matters

Most of Norkirk was built out from the 1940s through the 1970s, with some infill and teardown rebuilds scattered throughout. What that means in practice is that you are mostly looking at smaller lots, modest square footage by current Eastside standards, and homes that have been updated to varying degrees. Some have had full gut renovations. Others have had cosmetic updates but still have original windows, older electrical panels, or layouts that reflect how people lived fifty years ago.

That age range is not a problem if you go in with eyes open. It does mean inspections matter more than usual, and it means you should have a realistic sense of what you would want to update and what it would cost. A house that looks clean and freshly painted can still have a lot of deferred work underneath.

Lot sizes in Norkirk tend to run smaller than what you would find further east in Kirkland or out in Redmond. Yards exist, but if you are expecting a large flat backyard for kids or a dog, you may need to look at other neighborhoods. What you are trading that for is location and walkability, and for a lot of buyers that trade makes complete sense.

## What It Costs Relative to Downtown Kirkland

Waterfront and near waterfront properties in Kirkland carry a significant premium, as you would expect. Norkirk is priced meaningfully below those properties while still being in the same general walkable area. You are not getting a water view from most of these streets, and that is reflected in the price.

Compared to Houghton or the Juanita area to the north, Norkirk tends to be priced at a premium because of the walkability and proximity to the downtown core. The tradeoff for buyers who go a mile or two north is usually more house and lot for the money but a car dependent lifestyle for most errands.

There have also been more teardown and new construction projects in Norkirk over the last several years as builders recognize that location value. So you will occasionally find a newer single family home that feels completely out of place next to a 1950s bungalow. That is just what happens in desirable close in neighborhoods over time.

## Getting Around From Norkirk

Kirkland does not have the same transit infrastructure as Seattle, so the honest answer is that most residents drive for anything outside of walking distance. For downtown Kirkland errands and restaurants, you do not need a car. For everything else, you do.

Getting to Bellevue from Norkirk is reasonably straightforward via I 405, though the stretch between Kirkland and Bellevue gets slow during peak commute hours. Getting to Seattle is more of a commitment. The SR 520 bridge is accessible but it is not a quick trip, and traffic on the bridge is a daily reality if you are commuting west.

For anyone working in Redmond or the Eastside tech corridor, Norkirk is genuinely well positioned. The drive to the Redmond tech campuses is manageable, and I 405 access to Bellevue and further south is as good as you are going to get from anywhere in Kirkland.

## Who Actually Lives Here

It is a mix. You have longtime residents who bought here before the area got expensive and have no intention of leaving. You have younger couples and families who want walkability and are willing to buy a smaller older home to get it. And you have buyers who specifically want to be able to walk to the waterfront, the farmers market, or dinner without getting in a car, and have made that a priority over square footage or lot size.

It is not a neighborhood for someone who wants a large new construction home with a three car garage and a big yard. Those buyers are better served looking in Sammamish, Issaquah, or the eastern edges of Redmond. Norkirk rewards buyers who value walkability and the specific energy of being close to a small but real downtown.

## A Few Things Worth Thinking About Before You Commit

Parking can get tight near the commercial parts of Market Street, especially on weekends when downtown Kirkland pulls a lot of foot traffic. If you are on a street close to the activity, that is part of the deal.

Some streets in Norkirk are quieter than others. The blocks immediately adjacent to Market Street see more through traffic. A block or two off that corridor, things get noticeably more residential. It is worth walking the specific block you are considering at different times of day, not just during a Sunday open house when everything feels calm.

School assignments in this area feed into the Lake Washington School District, which is well regarded on the Eastside. The specific elementary school assignment depends on the address, so if schools are a priority, verify the exact assignment for any home you are seriously considering rather than assuming.

## Common Questions

### Is Norkirk actually walkable or is it one of those neighborhoods where walkability is exaggerated?

It is genuinely walkable by Eastside standards. You can reach coffee shops, restaurants, and the downtown Kirkland waterfront on foot without it being a stretch. That said, it is not a city neighborhood where you can go car free. Grocery runs and anything outside the immediate area still require a car for most people.

### How old are the homes in Norkirk and what does that mean for buyers?

Most of the original housing stock dates from the 1940s through the 1970s. Some have been substantially renovated and some have not. That age range means inspections matter. Older electrical, aging roofs, original windows, and foundation issues are all things that show up in this era of construction. Budget for potential updates even if the home presents well cosmetically.

### Are there newer homes in Norkirk or is it all older stock?

There is a mix. Because of the location, builders have been buying and tearing down older homes for years, so you will find newer single family construction and some infill townhomes alongside the older bungalows. The newer homes tend to be priced significantly higher but come with modern construction and layouts.

### How does commuting to Seattle work from Norkirk?

Most people drive to SR 520 and cross the bridge. Traffic on 520 during peak hours is real, and the commute to Seattle takes longer than the mileage suggests. It is not an impossible commute, but if you are doing it five days a week you will feel it. The Eastside commute to Bellevue or Redmond is considerably easier.

### What is the price difference between Norkirk and actual waterfront Kirkland?

Waterfront and direct water view properties carry a substantial premium, often well over a million dollars more for comparable square footage. Norkirk gives you proximity to the water on foot without the waterfront price. It is one of the more cost effective ways to live close to downtown Kirkland, though cost effective is relative given overall Eastside prices.

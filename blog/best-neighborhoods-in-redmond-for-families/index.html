Best Redmond Neighborhoods for Families

By Jack Macdonald
Macdonald Group of Compass

Families moving to Redmond usually have the same short list of questions: Which schools are they zoned for? How long is the drive to work? Do the kids have somewhere to ride bikes or walk to a friend's house? Those are the right questions, and the answers vary more than people expect depending on which part of Redmond you end up in.

Redmond is not one uniform suburb. The west side near downtown and the lake feels different from the hillside neighborhoods to the east. Education Hill is not the same experience as Overlake. The commute situation for a Microsoft employee looks nothing like it does for someone driving to Seattle every day. So rather than give you a ranked list, here is an honest look at the neighborhoods families tend to gravitate toward and what each one actually involves.

Education Hill

This is the neighborhood families ask about most often, and there is a reason for it. Education Hill is a large, relatively established residential area in the north part of Redmond, north of downtown, with a mix of homes built primarily in the 1970s through 1990s. Lots tend to be generous compared to newer developments, streets are quieter, and the area has the kind of settled, neighborhood feel that parents with young kids are usually looking for.

The school connection is not just in the name. Redmond High School sits atop Education Hill and serves much of this area. Several elementary schools serve the neighborhood and are well regarded within the Lake Washington School District, which is consistently one of the stronger districts in the state. That said, attendance boundaries shift and have shifted in recent years, so always verify which school a specific address feeds using the district's school locator before making a decision. I have seen buyers assume a school assignment and end up surprised.

Perrigo Park and the trail connections nearby give kids outdoor space to burn energy close to home. The tradeoff is that Education Hill is not particularly walkable for errands or restaurants. You will drive to most things, which matters if you were hoping to walk to coffee on a Saturday morning.

Prices here tend to be lower than the west side of Redmond, which is part of the appeal. A family that wants square footage, a real yard, and a quieter street without paying Bridle Trails or downtown Kirkland prices often lands here.

Overlake

Overlake sits on the southwest side of Redmond, right up against the Microsoft campus. For families where one or both parents work at Microsoft or nearby tech companies, the commute appeal is obvious. Some people literally walk or bike to work. That proximity changes daily life in meaningful ways.

The housing stock is more varied here than it used to be. There are older ranch style homes from the 1960s and 1970s alongside newer townhomes and some denser residential development. Lots are generally smaller than Education Hill. Redmond Town Center is close by, which makes this one of the more walkable pockets of the city for day to day errands.

Families with school age kids should look at which elementary they are assigned to based on their specific street, since this area can feed different schools depending on where exactly you are. Lake Washington School District serves Overlake, so the district quality holds, but the individual school experience varies.

One real consideration: SR 520 traffic and the density of development in this corridor means it does not have the quiet residential feel of Education Hill or the neighborhoods further east. It suits families who prioritize convenience and commute over a larger yard and slower pace.

Grass Lawn

Grass Lawn is a neighborhood that often gets overlooked in these conversations, which is a little surprising because it checks a lot of boxes for families. It sits roughly between downtown Redmond and Education Hill, centered around Grass Lawn Park. That park is a legitimate asset: athletic fields, open green space, playground equipment. For families with kids who play recreational sports, it becomes part of your weekly routine almost immediately.

The housing here is a mix of older single family homes, some updated and some not, with the occasional newer infill build scattered in. It is not a flashy neighborhood. What it offers is accessibility. You are close to the Redmond Watershed trail system, reasonably close to downtown Redmond, and within reach of the Sammamish River Trail if cycling is part of your life.

Families who like the idea of walking or biking to a park without getting in a car will appreciate this location more than most. The school picture here feeds into the Lake Washington district, and several of the elementary schools serving this area have strong reputations, though again, verify your specific address.

Downtown Redmond and the Bear Creek Corridor

Downtown Redmond has changed noticeably over the past several years. There is more density, more dining, more activity. For families, it is not necessarily the first choice if what you want is a quiet cul de sac and a big backyard, but it is worth mentioning for a specific type of family.

If walkability and access to restaurants, community events, and the Saturday market on Willows Road matters to you, and you have kids old enough to appreciate that environment, downtown Redmond works. The Link light rail extension to downtown Redmond opened in May 2025, adding two new stations and making transit to Bellevue and the broader region genuinely usable, which changes the calculus for some households.

The Bear Creek area to the east of downtown has pockets of single family homes that feel more suburban in character. It backs up toward more rural territory and has a different pace than the downtown core. Some families find this area appealing precisely because it feels removed from everything but is not actually that far from it.

The Willows Road and Marymoor Area

Marymoor Park is one of the better family amenities in the entire eastside. The off leash dog area, the climbing wall, the velodrome, the summer concerts, the trail connections to the Sammamish River Trail: families use this park constantly. Living near it has genuine daily value, not just weekend value.

The neighborhoods along and near Willows Road are a mix of residential pockets that often get lumped in with Redmond or Kirkland depending on where exactly you are. Homes here vary widely in age and style. Some are older and show it. Others have been substantially updated. The proximity to both Marymoor and the trail network makes this area popular with active families.

Commute wise, Willows Road gives decent access to SR 520 and to the Microsoft and Amazon campuses. It is not the fastest route to Seattle, but it is manageable depending on your schedule.

What Actually Matters When You Are Comparing These Areas

School assignment is real and worth checking carefully. The Lake Washington School District is strong overall, but individual schools vary, and boundaries have moved. Do not rely on what a neighbor tells you or what an online map suggests. Look up the actual address on the district's boundary tool and confirm it.

Lot size matters more than people admit when they have kids. A 4,000 square foot lot sounds like a yard until you are actually in it. Education Hill and parts of the Bear Creek area tend to have larger lots than Overlake or the downtown adjacent neighborhoods.

The Microsoft commute has shaped Redmond's housing demand significantly. If that commute is not a factor for you, you have more flexibility to focus on other things. If it is, Overlake and the neighborhoods close to the SR 520 interchange will be on your list regardless of other preferences.

Trail access is genuinely part of daily life for a lot of Redmond families in a way it is not in every suburb. The Sammamish River Trail and the Redmond Watershed trails are used regularly, not just on weekends. Where you live relative to those connections affects how much you actually use them.

A Realistic Take on Price Differences

Broadly, the west side of Redmond closer to Bellevue and SR 520 tends to cost more than the eastern and northern parts. Education Hill is generally more affordable per square foot than Overlake or the Marymoor area. That said, Redmond as a whole is not cheap, and even the more affordable pockets are priced at a level that requires real financial planning.

Families comparing Redmond to Sammamish or Issaquah often find that those areas offer newer construction at similar or sometimes lower prices, with the tradeoff being longer commutes to Bellevue and Seattle. That is a legitimate comparison to make and worth thinking through honestly based on your actual daily schedule.

Common Questions

Is the Lake Washington School District really as good as people say?

It is consistently one of the top performing districts in Washington State, with strong test scores, a range of advanced coursework, and programs that families relocating from other parts of the country are generally impressed by. That said, there is real variation between individual schools within the district. Redmond High School and many of the elementary schools in Redmond have strong reputations, but it is worth looking at the specific school your address feeds rather than assuming the whole district is uniform.

How does the Microsoft commute affect which Redmond neighborhood makes sense?

If you or your partner works on the Microsoft main campus in Redmond, Overlake is as close as you get without living in employee housing. Some people genuinely walk or bike. Education Hill adds maybe 10 to 15 minutes by car depending on traffic. The neighborhoods near Marymoor are also reasonable. If Microsoft is not in the picture, the commute difference between neighborhoods matters less and you can prioritize other things like lot size or trail access.

Are there good parks and outdoor options for kids in Redmond, or do you have to drive to find them?

Redmond is actually pretty good on this front. Marymoor Park is a major regional park with open fields, trails, a climbing wall, and an off leash dog area. Grass Lawn Park is well maintained with athletic fields. The Sammamish River Trail runs through the city and connects to the Burke Gilman Trail. The Redmond Watershed Preserve has real trails for hiking. Depending on where you live, you may be able to reach some of these on foot or by bike, which is one of the things families here genuinely appreciate.

What is the difference between buying in Redmond versus nearby Sammamish for a family?

Sammamish tends to have newer construction, larger planned communities, and sometimes lower prices per square foot for comparable space. The tradeoff is a longer commute to Bellevue, Seattle, and even the Microsoft campus. Redmond gives you shorter commutes and more access to city level amenities, but older housing stock in many neighborhoods and generally tighter lots. Neither is better across the board. It depends on whether commute time or housing newness matters more to your household.

Will the light rail in downtown Redmond change property values or commute patterns?

The Link extension to downtown Redmond opened in May 2025, adding the Marymoor Village and Downtown Redmond stations to the 2 Line. It makes transit to Bellevue and the broader region meaningfully faster for people who can walk or drive to either station. Whether it significantly moves property values in already expensive neighborhoods is harder to predict. It is probably most useful for households where one person commutes by rail and another works locally. For families where both parents drive to eastside employers, the rail line is a nice to have rather than a game changer.

Bellevue Farmers Market: What to Know Before You Go

By Jack Macdonald
Macdonald Group of Compass

People ask me about the Bellevue Farmers Market more than you might expect. It comes up in buyer consultations surprisingly often, usually from someone moving from Seattle or another city where a weekly farmers market was part of their routine. They want to know if Bellevue has that. The short answer is yes, and it's genuinely good.

Where It Is and When It Runs

The market runs on Thursday afternoons, and for 2026 the regular season is May 21 through October 1, in the parking lot of Bellevue Presbyterian Church at 1717 Bellevue Way NE, just north of downtown. Hours are 3 to 7:30 p.m. through August and 3 to 7 p.m. in September, though the vendors with the most popular items tend to sell out before the closing bell, especially if it's a warm summer Thursday.

The location is easy to get to from most of Bellevue, and parking is simple: there's free parking right on site in the church lot, and the market is on nearby bus routes.

What You'll Actually Find There

This is a producer only market, which matters. It means the vendors are the people who actually grew or made the product, not resellers buying from a warehouse and marking it up. The Bellevue Farmers Market, a nonprofit, requires it of every vendor, and everything sold there comes from within Washington State, and you can tell the difference when you're there. When you ask a vendor how something was grown or what variety an apple is, they know the answer.

The produce selection is strongest in midsummer through early fall. Stone fruits from eastern Washington show up in July and August, corn and tomatoes peak around August, and fall brings winter squash, potatoes, and the best of the apple crop. In May and June, expect more greens, radishes, spring onions, and early strawberries.

Beyond produce, the market has a solid mix of:

  • Bread and baked goods from a handful of regular vendors who rotate through the season
  • Flowers, particularly dahlias later in the summer, which this region grows exceptionally well
  • Eggs, honey, and small batch preserves
  • A few prepared food stalls with hot options for eating on site
  • Occasionally mushrooms, which tend to attract a line

It's not the biggest market in the region. The University District Farmers Market in Seattle runs year round and has more vendors. But for Bellevue residents, the Thursday afternoon timing and the Bellevue Way location make it genuinely convenient, and the quality is consistently high.

The Thursday Timing: A Real Consideration

It's worth being honest that this is a weekday market, but the afternoon and early evening hours help a lot. If you work from home or have a flexible schedule, you can go early in the afternoon, and if you're commuting to Seattle or working a traditional office schedule, you can usually still make it after work if you head straight there.

The regular season is Thursdays only, but for 2026 the market has also scheduled two Saturday fall markets, on October 31 and November 21, from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m.

How It Fits Into the Neighborhood

The market itself isn't in Old Bellevue, but Old Bellevue's stretch of Main Street, with good coffee, a mix of restaurants, and a few independent shops, is a short drive south if you want to extend the outing. It doesn't feel like an outdoor mall the way some parts of Bellevue do. Old Bellevue has a more relaxed, neighborhood scale feel than the Bellevue Square corridor a few blocks away.

For buyers looking at homes in the 98004 zip code, which covers much of this area, proximity to the farmers market is something that actually comes up as a selling point, and reasonably so. Being able to walk or bike to the market on a Thursday afternoon is a real lifestyle feature, not just a talking point.

A Few Practical Tips

Bring cash. Most vendors take cards now, but a few still prefer cash, and if you're buying from multiple stalls, cash moves faster and avoids the occasional connectivity issues you get with mobile readers at outdoor events.

Bring bags. Reusable bags or a sturdy tote make a real difference, especially if you're picking up both produce and flowers.

Come early if there's something specific you want. The vendors with artisan bread, popular flower arrangements, or specialty mushrooms can get picked over as the afternoon goes on. If you arrive in the last hour on a nice day, you'll still find plenty, but the selection won't be what it was right after the 3 p.m. opening.

The market has free parking right on site in the church lot, so you don't need a garage or street parking strategy.

Is There Anything Missing?

A few things the market doesn't have, at least not consistently: a full winter season. The regular Thursday season wraps up in early October, and after that there are only a couple of Saturday fall markets. The market does list fresh meats and seafood among what its vendors sell, but if you want more vendors or a market that runs every week of the year, the University District Farmers Market in Seattle is open Saturdays year round.

None of that is a reason to skip it. It just means it works best as one part of how you shop rather than a single stop for everything.

Common Questions

Is the Bellevue Farmers Market open year round?

No. For 2026 the regular season runs Thursdays from May 21 through October 1, plus two Saturday fall markets on October 31 and November 21. There's no regular winter season, so if you want a year round option, the University District Farmers Market in Seattle operates every Saturday regardless of season.

Is this a producer only market, or do vendors resell other farms' products?

It is a producer only market, which means vendors are required to sell what they grew, raised, or made themselves. The Bellevue Farmers Market, a nonprofit, requires it, and everything sold there comes from within Washington State. In practice it means better quality and more knowledgeable vendors, since you're talking directly to the grower.

Can you walk to the market from the Bellevue downtown core?

The market is at 1717 Bellevue Way NE, north of Bellevue Square, so it's walkable from the north end of downtown and a longer walk from Main Street and Old Bellevue. If you drive, there's free parking on site. From neighborhoods like Medina or Clyde Hill, most people drive.

What time of year has the best produce selection?

August through early October is the strongest window. That's when eastern Washington stone fruits are winding down, corn and tomatoes are peaking, and the early apple varieties start appearing. Dahlias are also at their best in late summer, if flowers are what you're after.

Is there food you can eat on site, or is everything meant to be taken home?

There are usually a few prepared food vendors with hot items, enough to make a meal of it if you want to eat while you browse. The surrounding Old Bellevue restaurants are also a natural extension of the visit if you want a proper sit down meal after shopping.

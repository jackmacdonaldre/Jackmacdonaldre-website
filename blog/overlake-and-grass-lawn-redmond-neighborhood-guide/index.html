Overlake and Grass Lawn Redmond: What to Expect

By Jack Macdonald
Macdonald Group of Compass

A lot of people searching in Redmond end up in Overlake or Grass Lawn almost by accident. They start looking in Bellevue, the prices push them east, and suddenly these two neighborhoods are on the list. That is not a bad thing. These are genuinely useful places to live if you understand what you are getting and what you are trading away.

Where These Neighborhoods Actually Are

Overlake straddles the Redmond and Bellevue border, which causes some confusion. The Redmond side of the Overlake neighborhood is in the southwest corner of the city, roughly bounded by SR 520 and NE 20th Street to the south, 148th Avenue NE to the west, and West Lake Sammamish Parkway and Bellevue Redmond Road to the east, with NE 60th Street forming the northern edge where it meets Grass Lawn, give or take. Grass Lawn sits just north of there, centered roughly around Grass Lawn Park on 148th Avenue NE. Per the City of Redmond, Grass Lawn runs from NE 60th Street on the south up to Redmond Way on the north, with 132nd Avenue NE on the west and SR 520 and West Lake Sammamish Parkway on the east. They are adjacent neighborhoods with different personalities, though people often lump them together when searching online.

The Housing Stock

Most of what you will find here was built between the late 1960s and the mid 1980s. Split levels, ramblers, and two story colonials on modest lots. These are not sprawling yards. Many lots run 7,000 to 9,000 square feet, sometimes smaller. The bones are solid but the homes have often been through multiple owners and varying levels of updating, so condition varies a lot from house to house.

What has changed in the last decade is teardown activity and ADU construction. You will see newer craftsman style builds mixed in among the originals. Some of the older ramblers have been split into rental configurations or had detached units added. That is worth paying attention to when you are evaluating a block, because the character of a street can shift significantly from one end to the other.

Grass Lawn tends to have slightly larger lots and more of the original single family character intact compared to Overlake proper. If a bigger yard matters to you, Grass Lawn is the better bet of the two.

Living Next to the Tech Corridor

The proximity to Microsoft's main campus on 148th Avenue NE is the biggest driver of demand here. If you or your partner works in Redmond tech or at one of the campuses along SR 520, you can sometimes walk or bike to work from these neighborhoods. That is a real quality of life factor that does not show up in Redfin estimates.

The tradeoff is that this proximity also means traffic on the main arterials gets genuinely bad during commute hours, especially on 148th and NE 24th. If you are driving west on SR 520 toward Seattle in the morning, budget your time accordingly. The highway on ramps in this area can back up early. The Overlake Village and Redmond Technology stations on the 2 Line are open and do matter for car free commuting options.

Grass Lawn Park and What It Actually Offers

Grass Lawn Park is worth knowing about if you are looking in this area. It is a legitimate neighborhood park, not a pocket park. At over 28 acres, there are athletic fields, tennis and basketball courts, a playground, a splash pad, and enough open space that it feels like a real amenity rather than just a green dot on the map. It draws youth sports leagues and pick up games regularly. Families with kids who play sports tend to notice this quickly.

The Sammamish River Trail, which connects at its northern end to the Burke Gilman Trail, is also reasonably close, which opens up biking and running options that go well beyond the neighborhood itself.

Schools

Both neighborhoods fall within the Lake Washington School District, which is well regarded and one of the things buyers ask about immediately when they look in this part of Redmond. Elementary school boundaries here have shifted over the years, so you should verify current assignments directly with the district rather than assuming based on what a neighbor says or what an older listing shows. Use the district's School and Bus Finder tool at lwsd.org to look up the specific address. The specific school your address feeds to matters, and it is worth confirming before you make an offer.

What You Are Paying For and What You Are Not

These neighborhoods offer access to excellent schools, short tech commutes, and established streets with mature trees, without the price premium of central Bellevue or the Redmond neighborhoods closer to downtown. That is the core value proposition.

What you are not getting is walkability in the traditional sense. There is no main street to walk to dinner or browse shops. Overlake Village has been developing on the Bellevue side, with retail and apartments going in, and that will eventually make the western edge of this area feel more connected. But right now, you are driving for groceries and most errands. This is not a walk to dinner kind of neighborhood, so check what grocery options are currently open near any address you are seriously considering.

You are also not getting new construction pricing. Older homes in this area sometimes carry deferred maintenance that is not obvious on a first showing. A thorough inspection is not optional here, it is essential. Pay particular attention to roofs, sewer lines (many original homes here have older cast iron or clay tile laterals), and any signs of additions or structural changes that may not have pulled permits.

Who Actually Does Well Here

Tech workers who want a short commute and a real house with a yard. Families who value the school district and do not need to be within walking distance of everything. People who have looked at comparable homes in Bellevue and are willing to cross 148th to get more for their money. Investors looking at rental potential given the proximity to Microsoft, though that market has gotten more competitive.

People who want a walkable urban feel tend to find these neighborhoods frustrating. So do buyers who want newer construction without paying a teardown premium. And if a large lot is important, you will need to be patient, they exist here but they are not the norm.

How These Two Stack Up Against Each Other

The short version: Overlake is more convenient and closer to SR 520 and the tech campuses, but Grass Lawn tends to have more neighborhood feel and slightly more elbow room. Overlake is also seeing more infill development and apartment construction on its edges, which changes the texture of some blocks. Grass Lawn is a bit quieter and more residential.

Price differences between the two are not dramatic, but Grass Lawn homes with larger lots can command a premium. The gap is usually meaningful enough to know which neighborhood you actually want before you start touring seriously.

Common Questions

Is Overlake in Redmond or Bellevue?

Both, depending on which block you are on. The Overlake area spans the Redmond and Bellevue city limits, which is why you will see listings on the same street attributed to different cities. If your address is Redmond, your property taxes and city services go through Redmond. If it matters to you, check the city boundary directly rather than relying on how a listing is labeled.

How close is the light rail to these neighborhoods?

The Overlake Village and Redmond Technology stations on the 2 Line are within reasonable distance of these neighborhoods, particularly for residents on the western and southern edges. Whether it is walkable or requires a short drive or bus connection depends on exactly where your house is. If using light rail is important to your commute plan, map the specific walk from the address you are considering before you assume it works for you.

Are the lots big enough for a detached ADU or garage conversion?

Sometimes yes, sometimes no. Lot sizes here are not particularly generous, and setback requirements will limit what is possible on the smaller parcels. Some homeowners have successfully added detached units or converted garages, but it requires checking city of Redmond zoning rules for the specific parcel. Do not assume an ADU is feasible without having someone run the numbers on that specific lot.

What is the sewer situation on older homes in this area?

Older homes in Overlake and Grass Lawn, particularly those built before the mid 1980s, may have original sewer laterals that have never been inspected or replaced. Clay tile and older cast iron lines can deteriorate or root intrude over time. A standard home inspection usually does not include the sewer. For homes of this age, a separate sewer scope is worth the cost. It is not a reason to walk away automatically, but it is something you want to know before closing.

How does traffic on 148th Avenue NE affect daily life?

During peak morning and evening commute times, 148th can get significantly congested, especially near the Microsoft campus entrances and the SR 520 interchange. If you are commuting westbound toward Seattle in the morning, the on ramp backups can add meaningful time. If you work locally or have flexibility in your schedule, it matters less. It is worth driving the route yourself at rush hour before deciding.

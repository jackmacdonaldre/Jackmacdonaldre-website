## Somerset Bellevue: What It's Actually Like to Live Here

Somerset sits on the southeast side of Bellevue, up in the hills above I 90, and it has a character that's hard to replicate closer to downtown. The lots are generous. The tree cover is real, not decorative. And on a clear day, the views from the upper streets reach across Lake Washington toward Seattle in a way that stops people mid sentence when they first see it.

I've been through these neighborhoods more times than I can count, and Somerset is the kind of place where people tend to stay. You don't see a lot of quick flips here. When a home comes available, neighbors notice, because turnover is low enough that new listings actually mean something.

## The Homes in Somerset

The housing stock in Somerset leans toward mid century and early 1980s construction, with a mix of split levels, traditional two story homes, and some more contemporary builds that have come in on the larger lots over the past decade or so. Lot sizes tend to be bigger than what you find in neighborhoods closer to downtown Bellevue, which is part of the draw for families who want space for a yard, a garden, or just some separation between themselves and their neighbors.

Many of the homes have been updated over the years, but the neighborhood still has a settled, established feel rather than the freshly constructed look you get in newer Issaquah or Sammamish developments. If you're coming from somewhere like Redmond or Kirkland and want more square footage without crossing into the far east suburbs, Somerset is often worth a serious look.

## Somerset Hill Park and Getting Outside

Somerset Hill Park is the anchor green space for the neighborhood, and it gets real use. The trails connect into a broader network that lets you get out on foot without jumping in a car, which matters more than people give it credit for when they're deciding where to live. On weekends you'll find families with kids, people walking dogs, and the occasional runner who figured out that the elevation here makes for a harder workout than it looks on a map.

The parks and trail access, combined with the views, are the two things I hear people mention most when they describe why they moved to Somerset and why they've stayed.

## Schools in the Somerset Area

Somerset falls within the Bellevue School District, which is one of the stronger public school districts in the state. Families moving to Bellevue consistently ask about schools first, and this part of the city holds up well under that scrutiny. I'd encourage anyone with specific questions about current school boundaries or programs to verify directly with the district, since boundaries and offerings do change, but the district reputation is something I hear reinforced regularly from the families I work with across Bellevue.

## Getting Around from Somerset

The location on the southeast side of Bellevue puts you close to I 90, which is a real practical advantage. Seattle is accessible without having to thread through downtown Bellevue traffic. Eastgate and the broader Bellevue grid are nearby, and Issaquah is a reasonable drive east if you need it. The trade, off is that this part of Bellevue is more car dependent than neighborhoods closer to the Spring District or downtown core. That's worth knowing going in rather than discovering later.

## What the Somerset Real Estate Market Looks Like

Somerset doesn't produce a huge volume of listings at any given time, which means when something comes up that fits, the window to act is usually shorter than buyers expect. Homes here compete on views, lot size, and school district access, and well priced properties tend to move. If you're tracking this neighborhood, it helps to be ready rather than getting ready after you find something you like.

Pricing varies significantly based on whether the home has views, how much the interior has been updated, and lot size. I don't think it serves anyone well to quote a number here that might be stale by the time someone reads it. What I can say is that Somerset consistently holds its value relative to comparable Bellevue neighborhoods, and the people I've helped buy here have not regretted the decision.

## Common Questions

### What makes Somerset different from other Bellevue neighborhoods?

Somerset sits up in the hills on the southeast side of Bellevue, which gives it larger lots, more tree cover, and views that many flatter neighborhoods simply don't have. It also tends to have lower turnover than neighborhoods closer to downtown, which reflects how much people like living there once they settle in.

### Is Somerset a good neighborhood for families?

It's one of the more popular choices for families in Bellevue. The Bellevue School District is a consistent draw, the parks and trails give kids somewhere to actually go, and the lots are large enough that homes feel like homes rather than townhomes with grass.

### How often do homes come up for sale in Somerset Bellevue?

Somerset has relatively low turnover compared to newer developments in Sammamish or Issaquah. That means fewer listings at any given time, and the ones that do come available tend to attract real interest quickly. If you're serious about this neighborhood, being financially prepared before you start looking matters.

### What schools serve the Somerset neighborhood?

Somerset falls within the Bellevue School District. I recommend verifying current school boundaries directly with the district since assignments can change, but the district overall is one of the stronger public school systems in Washington State.

### How is the commute from Somerset to Seattle?

Access to I 90 is one of Somerset's practical strengths. Seattle is reachable without fighting through downtown Bellevue. That said, this part of Bellevue is car dependent, so public transit options are more limited than you'd find near downtown Bellevue or the Spring District.

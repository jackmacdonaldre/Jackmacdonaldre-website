# Buying on the Sammamish Plateau: What to Know First

> A real local guide to Sammamish Plateau homes, neighborhoods, commute realities, schools, and tradeoffs from an agent who grew up in the area.

Author: Jack Macdonald, REALTOR, Macdonald Group of Compass, Bellevue WA
Published: August 21, 2026
Area: Sammamish, Washington
Web page: https://jackmacdonaldre.com/blog/sammamish-plateau-homes-for-sale-neighborhood-guide/

A lot of buyers come to me asking about the Sammamish Plateau after hearing it has good schools, newer homes, and more space than you'd get closer to Bellevue. All of that is generally true. But the Plateau is also a specific kind of place, and it works better for some people than others. This guide is meant to give you an honest picture so you can decide if it fits what you're looking for.

## What "the Plateau" Actually Means

Sammamish sits on an elevated shelf of land east of Lake Sammamish, bordered roughly by Redmond to the north and Issaquah to the south. When people say "the Plateau," they usually mean the incorporated city of Sammamish, which became its own city in 1999. Most of the housing there was built between the mid 1980s and the 2010s, with a big wave of development happening in the 1990s and 2000s as the tech industry expanded eastward.

The result is a city that feels cohesive in some ways and sprawling in others. There's no real downtown, no main street with restaurants and coffee shops within walking distance of most neighborhoods. The commercial areas are concentrated along Issaquah, Pine Lake Road, around the Sammamish Town Center (which is still somewhat in development as a destination), and near the edges of the city where it connects to Redmond and Issaquah. If you picture a walkable urban neighborhood, the Plateau is not that.

## The Housing Stock: What You're Actually Looking At

Most homes on the Plateau are single family houses on lots ranging from around 5,000 square feet up to a quarter acre or more, depending on the neighborhood and when it was built. Earlier subdivisions from the late 1980s and early 1990s tend to have more modest lots and simpler exteriors. Homes built in the late 1990s and 2000s often run larger, with two car garages, formal dining rooms, and the kind of floor plan that was standard for that era: living room, family room, kitchen that opens to the back, primary suite upstairs.

You'll also find newer construction, particularly in the northern part of the city near Redmond, where infill developments and some planned communities have added townhomes and paired homes that attract buyers who want less maintenance without leaving the area entirely.

Lot sizes do matter here because they vary more than people expect. Some neighborhoods back up to wetlands or greenbelt, which gives you privacy but also limits what you can do with the yard. Others are tightly packed enough that you can hear your neighbors through the fence. Worth paying attention to when you're comparing listings.

## Neighborhoods Within Sammamish Worth Knowing

The Plateau covers a lot of ground, and not all of it feels the same.

**Trossachs and Klahanie** are two of the more established neighborhoods in the southern end of the city. Klahanie has a community association, trails, parks, and a shopping center with groceries and restaurants, which makes it feel more self contained than a lot of the Plateau. Trossachs sits nearby and tends to attract buyers who want larger lots and a slightly quieter setting. These areas feed into the Issaquah School District rather than Lake Washington School District, which matters to some buyers and not at all to others.

**Pine Lake** is a mid Plateau area centered around Pine Lake itself, with some homes that back to the water or are close enough to use the park there. It's a mix of older and newer construction and tends to be a bit more affordable than some of the newer build neighborhoods.

**Newer communities** in the northern part of the city sit closer to Redmond. These developments from the 2000s and 2010s have more contemporary finishes and tend to be priced higher. The tradeoff is that you're farther from the southern retail clusters and closer to the Redmond commute corridors.

**Inglewood Hill Road area** is worth mentioning for buyers who want something that feels a bit more rural. There are larger parcels out there, some with acreage, and it's a different feel from the dense subdivisions closer to the city center.

## The Schools Question

Schools drive a lot of the demand on the Plateau, so it's worth being precise about this rather than just saying the schools are good.

Sammamish is split mainly between two districts. The northern part is in the Lake Washington School District, whose high school on the Plateau is Eastlake High School. Much of the southern part, including Klahanie and Trossachs, is in the Issaquah School District, which includes Skyline High School in Sammamish. A small area on the east edge is in the Snoqualmie Valley School District. All of them have strong reputations in the region. Either way, you're in solid district territory, but the specific school your kids would attend depends on the address, and it's worth confirming that before you fall in love with a particular house.

## Commuting Off the Plateau

This is the part of the Sammamish conversation that deserves the most honest treatment. Getting on and off the Plateau is genuinely difficult during peak hours, and it has been for years.

The main routes down are 228th Avenue NE toward Redmond, SE 8th Street and similar corridors toward Bellevue, and the various ways down into Issaquah toward I 90. None of them are fast at 7:30 in the morning. The roads that exit the Plateau funnel a lot of traffic down fairly limited arterials, and there's no light rail or meaningful transit option that changes that picture right now.

If you're commuting to Redmond, Microsoft's campus is relatively close in distance, but the actual drive time is highly variable. If you're going to Bellevue or Seattle, add meaningful time, and plan around it. Remote or hybrid work has made the Plateau more livable for people who were previously grinding that commute five days a week. If you're back in the office full time, be honest with yourself about whether the drive fits your life.

## What People Trade Off to Live Here

Buyers who choose the Plateau are usually making a conscious decision to prioritize space, schools, newer construction, and a quieter residential environment over walkability, urban access, and commute convenience. That's a legitimate set of priorities for a lot of families. The neighborhoods are generally safe, the parks are well maintained, and there's a strong sense of community in many of the established subdivisions.

The things you give up: you will drive everywhere. Groceries, coffee, dinner, kids' activities, all of it involves getting in the car. The Town Center has been developing slowly and has added some options, but it's not a substitute for real urban walkability. And if you value proximity to Seattle for weekend life as much as weekday work, the Plateau puts some distance between you and that.

Price is the other honest variable. The Plateau commands a premium because of the schools and the housing quality, but pricing has moved around since the 2022 peak, so lean on recent comparable sales rather than headlines. Compared to Bellevue or Kirkland, you generally get more house for the money. Compared to Issaquah just to the south, the gap is smaller, and it varies a lot by neighborhood and home.

## A Few Practical Details That Actually Matter

The Plateau gets real weather in winter. Snow doesn't happen constantly, but when it does, getting off the hill gets complicated quickly. Worth thinking about if you have any kind of regular obligation that can't flex.

HOA situations vary significantly by neighborhood. Klahanie has an active one. Some neighborhoods have minimal or no HOA involvement. If HOA fees or rules matter to you in either direction, check each community individually rather than assuming.

Lots with wetland buffers can complicate additions or outbuildings. If you're buying with plans to expand, confirm the setbacks before you commit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Sammamish a good place to buy if I'm not in tech?

Yes. The city skews toward tech employment because of proximity to Microsoft and Amazon's Eastside offices, but the neighborhoods themselves are just neighborhoods. Plenty of residents commute to healthcare, trades, education, and other industries. The commute math is the same regardless of your employer; it's more about direction and distance than industry.

### How different are the northern and southern ends of the city?

More different than people expect. The northern end near Redmond runs newer on average, tends to be priced a bit higher, and has better access to Redmond's amenities. The southern end around Klahanie and Trossachs has a more established feel, larger trees, and connects more naturally toward Issaquah and I 90. School district boundaries also split along roughly this north, south line. If you have a strong preference for one district over the other, let that narrow your search geographically first.

### Are there condos or attached homes on the Plateau?

Not many. The Plateau is overwhelmingly single family detached housing. There are some townhome developments, particularly in newer pockets near the Redmond border, and a small number of condo communities, but this is not a place with a deep inventory of attached product. If you need that price point or that format, Issaquah, Redmond, or Bellevue will give you more options.

### What's the wildfire or natural hazard situation?

The Plateau itself is less exposed than more rural areas to the east, but it's not without risk. Some neighborhoods near forested edges or open space carry more fire exposure than dense subdivisions. Flooding isn't a widespread concern on the elevated portions of the Plateau, but properties near wetlands or creek corridors can have drainage issues. Worth a standard inspection and a look at any wetland designation on the parcel before closing.

### Do homes here tend to have good resale history?

Generally yes. The combination of school district quality and housing type has supported consistent demand over time. That said, the Plateau was not immune to the broader correction that hit the Eastside in 2022 and into 2023, and some buyers who purchased at peak 2021 and early 2022 prices are sitting on paper losses if they needed to sell quickly. Longer holds have done well historically, but short term timing risk is real here as it is anywhere.

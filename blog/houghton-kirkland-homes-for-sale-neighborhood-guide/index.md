# Houghton Kirkland: What Buyers Actually Need to Know

> Thinking about Houghton in Kirkland? Here is what the neighborhood actually looks like, who it suits, and what to expect from the housing market.

Buyers ask me about Houghton fairly often, usually because they did a search, saw some interesting homes, and then realized they had no mental picture of where it actually sits or what day to day life there looks like. It is a fair starting point. Houghton is not the kind of Kirkland neighborhood that gets written about constantly, but the people who buy there tend to stay a long time, which tells you something.

## Where Houghton Actually Is

Houghton sits in the southern part of Kirkland, roughly between downtown Kirkland to the north and Bellevue to the south. Lake Washington Boulevard runs along its western edge. The neighborhood has its own small commercial district along NE 68th Street where you will find a grocery store, coffee shops, a handful of restaurants, and everyday services within a short drive or walk for many residents. It does not have the pedestrian buzz of downtown Kirkland, but it is genuinely convenient in a lower key way.

The proximity to SR 520 is real and meaningful. If you work in Seattle, Houghton is one of the more practical Kirkland locations for that commute. You can get to the 520 bridge without dealing with as much of the Kirkland grid as you would from Juanita or Totem Lake.

## The Housing Stock

Most of the homes in Houghton were built in the 1950s through the 1970s, with a solid mix of ramblers, split levels, and two story homes on established lots. The lots tend to be generous by Eastside standards, often 7,000 to 10,000 square feet or more, with mature trees and landscaping that gives the neighborhood a settled, shaded feel that newer subdivisions just do not have.

You will also find newer construction mixed in, either rebuilds or infill projects, particularly on some of the streets closer to the water. The contrast can be stark. A modernized 1960s rambler on a big lot might sit next door to a 2018 build. For some buyers that variety is fine, for others who want uniformity it is worth knowing upfront.

Waterfront and water view homes exist in Houghton, and they command a significant premium over the non waterfront inventory. Some of the most desirable Lake Washington properties in all of Kirkland are in this neighborhood. That said, the majority of the housing stock is standard residential, not waterfront, and it prices accordingly.

## What Draws People Here

A lot of it comes down to location without the downtown premium. You are close to the Kirkland Urban shopping center, close to the Cross Kirkland Corridor trail, and you have reasonable access to both Bellevue and Seattle. Buyers who want that coverage and cannot stomach the prices closer to downtown Kirkland often land in Houghton and find it suits them well.

The neighborhood also has a relatively calm, residential feel once you get off the main arterials. Streets like 7th Avenue and around the Houghton Beach Park area are genuinely quiet. Houghton Beach Park itself is a small but well used spot on the lake with a swimming area and views of Seattle and Lake Washington that make the location feel distinctly Pacific Northwest.

## Schools

Houghton sits within the Lake Washington School District, which is consistently one of the stronger districts on the Eastside. The specific school assignments depend on your address, so it is worth verifying during your search rather than assuming. Buyers with kids in elementary and middle school tend to do their homework on this early, and I would encourage that regardless of what you hear generally about the district.

## What the Tradeoffs Look Like

Houghton is not the most walkable neighborhood in Kirkland. You can walk to the small commercial area on NE 68th Street from parts of the neighborhood, but a lot of residents drive for most errands. If walkability is a top priority, downtown Kirkland is more appropriate, though you will pay for it.

Some of the older homes have deferred maintenance issues that are easy to overlook when the setting is beautiful and the lot is large. A 1960s split level on a tree covered lot can photograph very well and still have old electrical, galvanized plumbing, or a furnace that is running on borrowed time. In a competitive offer situation there is sometimes pressure to waive inspection, but in Houghton specifically, given the age of the housing stock, that is a decision worth thinking hard about.

Traffic on Lake Washington Boulevard during peak hours can back up noticeably, especially in summer when recreational traffic adds to the commuter load. If you are in the western part of the neighborhood, that is just part of the reality.

## How Houghton Compares to Nearby Parts of Kirkland

Compared to downtown Kirkland, Houghton is quieter and less walkable but offers more land and often more house for the price. Compared to Juanita to the north, Houghton tends to skew a bit higher in price because of the location and the lake proximity. Compared to Rose Hill or Totem Lake, it feels more established and the lots are larger, but you are trading some of the newer construction and easy freeway access those areas offer.

The buyers who fit Houghton best are usually people who want space, a mature neighborhood feel, reasonable commute options, and do not need to be able to walk to a bar on a Friday night. It rewards people who know what they want.

## Common Questions

### Is Houghton actually a distinct neighborhood or just part of Kirkland generally?

Houghton has its own identity and is recognized as a distinct neighborhood within Kirkland. It has its own small commercial area, its own park on the lake, and longtime residents tend to identify with the name specifically rather than just saying Kirkland.

### Are there condos or townhomes in Houghton, or is it mostly single family homes?

It is predominantly single family homes. There is some attached housing closer to the commercial corridors, but the character of the neighborhood is really defined by the single family residential streets. Buyers looking for condos have more options in downtown Kirkland or along the Totem Lake corridor.

### How realistic is it to get waterfront in Houghton, and what does it cost?

Waterfront homes do come up in Houghton, but not frequently, and the prices reflect how rare they are. Lake Washington waterfront anywhere on the Eastside commands a substantial premium, and Houghton is no exception. Most buyers who end up in Houghton are buying non waterfront homes, sometimes with partial water views depending on elevation and lot position.

### What is the Cross Kirkland Corridor and how does it factor into living here?

The Cross Kirkland Corridor is a crushed gravel multi use trail that runs along a former rail line through Kirkland. Parts of it are accessible from Houghton and connect to a broader trail network. For people who bike, run, or just want a car free way to move around, it is a genuine amenity. It does not replace walkability to shops, but it adds recreational value to the neighborhood.

### Do homes in Houghton tend to sell quickly or sit on the market?

It varies by price point and condition. Well priced, updated homes in Houghton move fast when inventory is low. Homes that need significant work or are priced optimistically can sit. The neighborhood has enough demand that pricing it right matters a lot, more so than in areas with less competition.

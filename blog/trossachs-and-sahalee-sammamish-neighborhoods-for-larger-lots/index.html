Trossachs and Sahalee: Sammamish Lots Worth Knowing

By Jack Macdonald
Macdonald Group of Compass

One of the most common things I hear from buyers who've been looking in Sammamish for a while is some version of: "I keep seeing these big lots on Zillow but they're all in the same two neighborhoods." Usually they're talking about Trossachs and Sahalee, even if they don't know the names yet. These two communities sit on the east side of Sammamish Plateau and both offer something genuinely harder to find up here: space between you and your neighbors.

That's not a small thing in Sammamish, where a lot of the newer construction from the 2000s and 2010s packed homes in fairly tight. If lot size matters to you, these neighborhoods deserve a closer look. But they're not interchangeable, and the differences are worth understanding before you start scheduling showings.

What Makes Trossachs Different

Trossachs is a planned community that developed mostly in the late 1990s through the early 2000s. The homes here are on larger lots by Sammamish standards, with lot sizes that vary across the different divisions of the neighborhood. Some portions run closer to a quarter acre or less, while other sections have more generous parcels, so it is worth looking at individual listings rather than assuming a consistent lot size across the whole development. The neighborhood has a Scottish theme going on with its street names, which is a minor curiosity more than anything meaningful.

Architecturally, Trossachs runs toward the traditional side. You'll see a lot of craftsman and colonial influenced homes, good sized yards, some with mature trees. The community has a neighborhood association, trails, parks, and a generally maintained feel. It's the kind of place where people stay for a long time, which means turnover is lower and you won't always find a lot of active listings.

Trossachs sits on the southeast side of the Sammamish Plateau, and the main routes out connect south toward Issaquah and I 90, or north through the city toward Redmond and SR 202. Like anywhere on the Plateau, rush hour on the main corridors can slow things down.

Sahalee: Gated, Golf Course Adjacent, Different Price Point

Sahalee is a gated community, and that changes the whole character of the neighborhood. It's built around Sahalee Country Club, which hosts serious tournament golf and is one of the more well regarded private courses in the Pacific Northwest. The club has hosted the PGA Championship, the U.S. Senior Open, and multiple Women's PGA Championships. You don't have to be a club member to buy a home in Sahalee, but many residents are, and the community's identity is closely connected to it.

The lots here are generally larger than Trossachs. Some back directly onto the golf course, others are more wooded and private. The homes tend to be larger too, and the price range reflects it. Sahalee sits at the higher end of the Sammamish market. As of late 2026, homes in the gated community are often in the two million dollar range and above, with the premium properties pushing well past that.

The gated aspect is worth thinking about practically, not just as a prestige feature. Delivery drivers, contractors, visitors all go through the gate. That's something people either love for the privacy and security or find mildly annoying over time. Worth knowing before you fall for a house inside.

Sahalee is in the Lake Washington School District, which is an important distinction from much of the rest of Sammamish, which is split between Lake Washington School District and Issaquah School District. Parents researching schools should verify exactly which school a specific address in Sahalee is assigned to using the district's school locator, since that can be a deciding factor for some buyers.

How the Lots Actually Compare

Both neighborhoods offer more land than the typical Sammamish subdivision, but they serve different versions of that desire. In Trossachs, a larger lot often means a good sized usable backyard, room for a playset, a garden, maybe a shop or ADU down the road depending on zoning. The scale feels residential and practical.

In Sahalee, the lots can be very large but some of that land is wooded or on a slope, so the "usable" portion may be smaller than the total square footage suggests. That's not a complaint, it's just useful to know. If you want a flat acre to do something with, that's different from a wooded half acre on a ridge with views. Both have appeal, but they're different kinds of appeal.

Who Actually Ends Up in Each Neighborhood

Trossachs tends to attract families who want room, good schools, a connected neighborhood feel, and a reasonable but not extreme price premium over standard Sammamish inventory. It's a step up without being a leap.

Sahalee attracts buyers for whom privacy, prestige, the golf connection, and larger estates are actual priorities rather than nice to haves. The buyers tend to be further along professionally and are often specifically choosing the neighborhood rather than just landing there. If you're not planning to use the country club and don't particularly value the gated setup, you're paying for things that don't matter to you, which is worth being honest about.

A Few Practical Notes

Both communities are HOA governed, and in Sahalee the HOA is fairly active given the gated infrastructure and common areas. Budget for HOA dues and understand what they cover before making an offer.

Commute wise, both neighborhoods are on the Plateau, which means you're relying on the same arterial corridors as the rest of the area. From the southern end of the Plateau, the main route to I 90 runs south through Issaquah. The SR 202 corridor connects north toward Redmond, which makes it the more natural route for SR 520 and tech campus commuters. Neither is a short commute into Seattle, and the Plateau corridors do back up. If someone is commuting to Bellevue or Redmond tech campuses, it's more manageable. Amazon and downtown Seattle add significant time, especially westbound in the mornings.

Inventory in both neighborhoods moves slowly because turnover is low. When a house comes up in Sahalee especially, it can sit for a while at the high end of the market, or it can move quickly if it's priced right and the right buyer is looking. The pool of qualified buyers at that level is smaller, which means timing and pricing matter differently than in a higher volume neighborhood.

Common Questions

Do I need to be a Sahalee Country Club member to buy a home in Sahalee?

No, membership is separate from ownership. You can buy a home in Sahalee without joining the club. That said, many residents are members, and the community's character is shaped by that connection. Membership at Sahalee Country Club is by invitation only and comes with its own costs, so if golf is part of why you're interested, look into that process early.

Which school district do Trossachs and Sahalee feed into?

Trossachs is in the Issaquah School District. Sahalee is generally in the Lake Washington School District, which is based in Redmond and also serves parts of Sammamish and other nearby communities. That difference matters to families comparing the two neighborhoods, since the districts have different schools, programs, and boundaries. Always verify the specific address with the district's school locator before assuming, as boundary lines on the Plateau can be complicated.

Are the large lots in these neighborhoods actually usable or mostly wooded slope?

It depends on the specific lot. In Trossachs, most larger lots have a good portion of flat, usable yard. In Sahalee, there's more variation: some lots back onto the golf course and are very open, others are heavily treed with significant grade changes. When you're evaluating a property, look at the terrain in person rather than relying on the total lot square footage alone.

What's the price difference between Trossachs and Sahalee?

Sahalee runs considerably higher. Trossachs homes are typically in the range most buyers associate with higher end Sammamish inventory, while Sahalee starts at a noticeably elevated price point and goes up significantly from there, particularly for golf course or premium view lots. The gap reflects the gating, lot sizes, home scale, and overall positioning of the community. Prices change, so check current listings and recent sales data before drawing conclusions.

How hard is it to get in and out of these neighborhoods during commute hours?

Both sit on the Sammamish Plateau, which has no freeway running through it, so you're depending on a few key arterial corridors. From the southern end of the Plateau, the main route to I 90 runs south through Issaquah. The SR 202 corridor connects north toward Redmond and SR 520, which is the more direct option for tech campus commuters. Morning commutes toward Seattle or Bellevue can be slow. It's not unusable, but it's also not a quick drive in heavy traffic.

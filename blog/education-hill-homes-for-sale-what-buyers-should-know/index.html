Education Hill Homes for Sale: What Buyers Should Know

By Jack Macdonald
Macdonald Group of Compass

Education Hill in Redmond: A Neighborhood That Earns Its Reputation

Education Hill sits in the heart of Redmond, and if you have spent any time looking at homes in the Eastside, you have probably heard the name come up more than once. There is a reason for that. This is a neighborhood where families tend to stay, where neighbors actually know each other, and where the streets feel lived in rather than staged. That is not a coincidence. It is the product of good schools, walkable parks, and a location that makes everyday life genuinely easier.

I have been working the Eastside market for a long time, and Education Hill is one of those places I can talk about with some specificity, not just general enthusiasm.

What the Streets Actually Feel Like

Education Hill rises up from the flats of Redmond with views that catch you off guard if you are not expecting them. The terrain gives the neighborhood a distinct sense of place. The residential streets off 166th Ave NE and the blocks surrounding Hartman Park have a quieter residential feel that does not always match what buyers expect when they hear Redmond. Most people associate Redmond with tech campuses and new construction. Education Hill is something else. It is older tree cover, established lots, and a mix of ramblers, split levels, and updated Craftsman style homes that reflect decades of real use.

Hartman Park is one of the anchors here. It is a 40 acre park with baseball and turf fields, tennis and pickleball courts, trails, and the Redmond Pool, and it draws families from across the area. If you have kids who play any kind of organized sport, you will probably spend time there regardless of where you live in Redmond. Living within walking distance of it is a genuine quality of life upgrade, not a marketing line.

Schools and Why They Drive Demand

The neighborhood name is not accidental. Education Hill is served by the Lake Washington School District, which consistently draws strong reviews from families across the Eastside. I am careful not to quote ratings that shift year to year, but what I can tell you is that the schools in this area come up in almost every conversation I have with buyers considering Redmond. Parents ask about them early, and the answers tend to reinforce the interest rather than complicate it.

That reputation for strong public schools is one of the primary reasons homes on Education Hill hold their value well and tend to move quickly when priced right. Buyers know what they are getting, and they are willing to compete for it.

What the Market Looks Like for Buyers

Homes on Education Hill range widely depending on size, age, and condition. You will find original construction from the seventies and eighties alongside homes that have been substantially updated or rebuilt. Lot sizes tend to be generous by Eastside standards, which matters to buyers who want outdoor space without going all the way out to Sammamish or Woodinville.

Inventory here moves. When a well priced home comes up in this neighborhood, it does not sit. Buyers who are serious need to be prepared: financing lined up, priorities clear, and someone in their corner who understands the neighborhood well enough to help them read a listing correctly before they walk through the door.

That is the part of this job I take seriously. Knowing whether a home is priced to reflect the lot or the finishes or the school boundary it sits in requires more than a spreadsheet. It requires knowing the street.

Getting to Work from Education Hill

Redmond's location on the Eastside means commuters have real options. Access to SR 520 puts downtown Bellevue and Seattle within reach, and the local Microsoft and Amazon campuses are close enough that many residents can avoid the freeway entirely on their worst days. The Eastside's broader infrastructure continues to evolve, and Redmond is well positioned within it.

Is Education Hill Right for You

If you are a family prioritizing schools and park access, this neighborhood deserves a serious look. If you are a buyer who values established neighborhoods over brand new construction, you will find more character here than in many of the newer developments going up elsewhere on the Eastside. And if you are a homeowner thinking about selling, the demand for this area is real and consistent, which matters when it comes to timing and strategy.

I grew up on the Eastside and have watched these neighborhoods change over decades. Education Hill has held onto what makes it work while the areas around it have shifted considerably. That kind of stability does not happen by accident.

If you are thinking about buying or selling on Education Hill and want to talk through what the market looks like right now, reach out to me directly. I would rather have a real conversation about your specific situation than send you a generic report. That is how this works best.

Common Questions

What makes Education Hill in Redmond a popular neighborhood for families?

Education Hill is served by the Lake Washington School District, which has a strong reputation across the Eastside. The neighborhood is also home to Hartman Park, generous lot sizes relative to other Eastside areas, and a quieter residential feel that appeals to families who want space without moving far from Redmond's core.

How competitive is the real estate market on Education Hill?

Well priced homes on Education Hill tend to move quickly. Demand is driven by school district access and the neighborhood's established character. Buyers should come prepared with financing in order and a clear sense of priorities before they start touring.

What types of homes are available on Education Hill in Redmond?

The housing stock on Education Hill is a mix of original construction from the seventies and eighties, updated ramblers and split levels, and some substantially renovated or rebuilt homes. Lot sizes tend to be larger than in many newer Eastside developments, which is part of what draws buyers to the area.

How does Education Hill compare to other Redmond neighborhoods?

Education Hill has more established tree cover, older lots, and a quieter residential feel than some of the newer developments in Redmond. It sits at a slightly elevated position in the city, which gives parts of the neighborhood some views. It tends to appeal to buyers who want neighborhood character and school district access over brand new finishes.

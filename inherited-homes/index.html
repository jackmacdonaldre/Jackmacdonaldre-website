How an estate sale works

1. Start with who can sign

Before anything else, we confirm with your attorney who has the authority to sell: a personal representative, a trustee, or a beneficiary on a transfer on death deed. Getting this right first keeps the sale from stalling later.

2. Protect the house

Vacant homes need attention. I'll help you check the insurance, keep the utilities on, secure the property and keep an eye on it so nothing goes wrong while the family sorts things out.

3. Sort the belongings

Family first, then an estate sale company, donations and a final clean out. I'll help you line up the right people and the order of operations so it doesn't drag on for months.

4. Decide: fix up or sell as is

I'll walk the house with you and give you two honest numbers: what it could sell for as is, and what it could sell for after a short list of fixes, with conservative costs for each. You choose what makes sense for the estate.

5. Price it off real sales

I build the price from what actually sold nearby, and I show every heir the comps behind it, so the number isn't something anyone has to take on faith.

6. Sell and close

Photos, marketing, showings and negotiation, then I manage inspection, escrow and signing, including remote signing for family out of the area, all the way to closing.

Washington basics worth knowing

I'm not an attorney or a CPA, and every estate is different, so your attorney and CPA have the final word. These are the basics families ask me about most:

  • Nonintervention powers. When the court grants them, the personal representative can sell estate property without a court order (RCW 11.68.090).
  • Transfer on death deeds. If the owner recorded one, the home passes to the named beneficiary outside of probate.
  • Excise tax. Inheriting a home is not subject to Washington's real estate excise tax, but a sale from an heir to an outside buyer is (WAC 458-61A-202).
  • Tax basis. The IRS generally uses the home's fair market value on the date of death as the basis for inherited property.

Sources: RCW 11.68.090, WAC 458-61A-202, IRS: Gifts and inheritances, King County Law Library on transfer on death deeds. This page is general information, not legal or tax advice.

Common questions

Who can sign to sell a home that is part of an estate?

Usually the personal representative named by the court, sometimes called the executor. If the home was in a trust, the trustee signs, and if the owner recorded a transfer on death deed, the home goes to the named beneficiary outside of probate. Your estate attorney will confirm who has the authority, and I'll work around that from day one.

Do we need a judge to approve the sale in Washington?

Often not. When the court grants the personal representative nonintervention powers, Washington law lets them sell estate property without a court order. Your attorney will know whether you have those powers.

Should we fix the house up or sell it as is?

It depends on the house and on what the family wants. I'll walk through it with you and price both options: a short list of fixes that usually pay for themselves, or selling as is. Some families want the fastest, simplest path, and that's a fine choice too.

What do we do with everything inside?

Start with what the family wants to keep, then decide on the rest. An estate sale company can sell furniture and belongings, and whatever is left can be donated or hauled away. I can connect you with people my clients have liked and help you line up the timing so the house is ready to list.

Can you help if we live out of state?

Yes. A lot of families I talk to don't live nearby. I can check on the house, meet contractors and vendors, send photos and video updates, and set up signing so everyone can sign from where they are.

What will the sale mean for taxes?

That is a question for your CPA, but here is the basic idea. The IRS generally sets the basis of inherited property at its fair market value on the date of death, and Washington's real estate excise tax does not apply to the inheritance itself. A sale from an heir to an outside buyer is taxable. I can pull what similar homes sold for around the date of death to help that conversation.
